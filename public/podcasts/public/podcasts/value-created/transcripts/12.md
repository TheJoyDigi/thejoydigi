**Jordan:** Start with the definition, because people use this word loosely. Trust is the belief that the other person will do what they said they would do, even when nobody is watching and even when it costs them something. A Nobel Prize winning economist named Kenneth Arrow once wrote that virtually every commercial transaction has an element of trust in it, and that much of the economic backwardness in the world can be explained by the lack of mutual confidence. **Alex:** Think of trust as a tax, or a dividend, on every deal you ever do. Where trust is high, business is cheap. You shake hands, you sign a short contract, the work starts Monday. The price is double. Economists call those extras the transaction costs, and they are, quite literally, the bill for missing trust.

**Alex:** Now the deeper physics, because trust behaves like nothing else you own. It compounds, and it compounds in one direction only: slowly up, and instantly down. Every kept promise is a small deposit. Every deposit pays interest. And one broken promise can empty the account in a single afternoon. Behavioral scientists have run this as a game for decades. Two strangers, a little money, a choice to share it or take it. The pairs that share grow their pile round after round. The pairs that take end up with almost nothing. The math of cooperation is simple: trust is the only strategy that gets richer with time.
