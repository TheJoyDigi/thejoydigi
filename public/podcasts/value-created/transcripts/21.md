**Jordan:** Here is the sentence this chapter hangs on. Do one thing well, and trade for the rest. Everything else is commentary. The math is brutal and simple. You have twenty-four hours, and so does everyone else. If you try to do everything yourself, your output is capped by two things: your own time, and your own mediocrity at the things you are bad at. **Alex:** Watch it happen with two bakers. Elena and Priya open bakeries in the same year, on the same street, baking the same sourdough. Elena believes in doing everything herself. **Jordan:** So how do you actually do this? Start with the trade test. Look at everything on your plate and ask one question. Could someone else do this eighty percent as well as me, for less than my best hour is worth? If the answer is yes, trade it. Your best hour is the hour spent on the thing only you can do at your level. Every hour you spend on the other stuff is an hour you bought back at a discount and spent at full price. Most people fail this test in the same place. They say, but nobody does it as well as I do. True. The accountant will never love your books the way you do. The delivery kid will never care about your bread the way you do. That is not the standard. Eighty percent is the standard, because the twenty percent you lose on the traded task, you gain back ten times over on the thing you freed yourself to do. And here is the part people miss. Trade is not just hiring help. It is a strategy for what you become. Every trade is a vote for your specialty. The more you trade away, the sharper the one thing gets, because it gets all of your hours and all of your attention. Specialists are not born specialized. They become specialized by trading everything else away, one trade at a time. One warning. Do not trade the crown jewel. The rule is trade everything except the one thing. Priya the baker does not outsource the baking. The moment you trade away the thing customers actually pay for, you become a middleman with no edge, and middlemen get squeezed. So the trade test has a second question, and you ask it first. What is the one thing? Name it in one sentence. If you cannot name it, you do not have a specialty yet, and your first job is to find one, not to trade. But once you can name it, everything unnamed is negotiable. Start this week. Write down everything you did last week. Circle the one thing that created the most value. Everything else is a trade waiting to happen.
