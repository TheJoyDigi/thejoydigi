**Alex:** You land in a new city. You need a ride. You get in a stranger's car, at night, because an app shows a number. Four point nine.

**Jordan:** You sleep in a stranger's apartment because strangers left stars. You eat at a restaurant you have never seen because a thousand people you will never meet said it was good.

**Alex:** None of this worked thirty years ago. What changed? Someone built a machine that makes reputation visible. And it rewired trust itself.

**Alex:** Welcome to Incentives, Decoded. I am Alex.

**Jordan:** And I am Jordan. This is chapter twenty-four, Reputation Systems. Last time, in Skin in the Game, you learned to trust whoever shares the downside, because consequences cannot be faked. Today, we scale that idea. You cannot personally verify every driver, host, and restaurant. So we built systems that do it for you. Reviews, ratings, scores. Let us learn how they work, and when they break.

**Alex:** A reputation system is an incentive machine. It takes the oldest payoff in human history, what will people think of me, and makes it measurable, visible, and permanent. Behave well, get stars. Behave badly, lose them.

**Jordan:** Start with Uber. Five stars after every ride. Both sides rate each other.

**Alex:** It works because the stakes are real and immediate. Drivers whose rating drops too low, often around four point six, can be deactivated. That is not a bad review. That is unemployment. The incentive to be clean, safe, and polite is enormous.

**Jordan:** And it is hard to fake. You cannot buy a thousand real rides. Each rating is tied to a real transaction, a real person, a real car. The system connects reputation to reality, and that is why you trust it enough to get in the car.

**Alex:** Now compare Amazon reviews. Same five stars. Totally different machine. Sellers figured out that reviews drive sales, so they bought them. Thousands of fake five-star reviews, written by people who never touched the product.

**Jordan:** Amazon fights it constantly, because the incentive to fake is massive and the cost of faking is low. A fake review costs a few dollars. A top ranking earns thousands. The system is under permanent attack, and some categories are basically fiction now.

**Alex:** The difference is the cost of lying. Uber ties every rating to a verified ride. Amazon reviews are far easier to fake. Low-cost lies flood the system. High-cost lies stay rare.

**Jordan:** Here is the deeper insight. Reputation works when three things are true. One, identity is real. You know who is rating and who is being rated. Anonymous systems collapse, because there is no cost to lying.

**Alex:** Two. The rating is tied to a real transaction. Not an opinion. A thing that happened. Uber works because the ride happened. Credit scores work because the payments happened. Detach the rating from reality and it becomes theater.

**Jordan:** Three. There is no easy reset. If a bad actor can delete their account and start fresh, reputation is optional. The system only disciplines people who cannot escape it.

**Alex:** When all three hold, strangers trust strangers at scale. When any one breaks, you get noise. Fake reviews, brigading, astroturfing.

**Jordan:** So let us turn this around. How do you use reputation systems?

**Alex:** When you check reviews, study the three-star ones. Five stars are fans. One star is enemies. Three stars is someone thinking clearly.

**Jordan:** Do you check who is reviewing? Verified buyers beat unknowns. Locals beat tourists. The identity of the rater is half the signal.

**Alex:** And your own reputation. Your Uber rating. Your credit score. Your professional name. Every system is keeping score. Are you building it on purpose?

**Jordan:** Because in a reputation economy, your stars are your track record. Reputation compounds. Neglect compounds too.

**Alex:** So here is the move. Whether you are building a system or borrowing one.

**Jordan:** One. Make reputation visible. Show the score. People behave better when they know they are being rated and the rating sticks.

**Alex:** Two. Make it hard to fake. Tie every rating to a verified transaction. Require identity. Prevent resets. Spend your design effort on the cost of lying.

**Jordan:** Three. Borrow trust wisely. Read the three-star reviews. Check the rater's identity. Discount anything that could be bought.

**Alex:** Reputation is the closest thing we have to scalable trust. Protect the machine, and it protects you.

**Jordan:** Stars are just incentives made visible.

**Alex:** Make sure yours are earned.
