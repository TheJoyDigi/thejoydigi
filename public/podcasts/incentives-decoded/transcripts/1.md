**Alex:** Their incentive is your attention. They get paid when you scroll. So they built machines that make you scroll. And it works. On you, on me, on everyone.

**Jordan:** That is not a willpower problem. That is an incentive problem. And once you learn to spot it, you will see it running everywhere.

**Alex:** Welcome to Incentives, Decoded. I am Alex.

**Jordan:** And I am Jordan. This is chapter one, The Iron Law. Today you get a lens you can point at almost any confusing human behavior, and it will snap into focus.

**Alex:** The lens is one sentence, and it comes from Charlie Munger, Warren Buffett's partner. He said it like this. Show me the incentive, and I will show you the outcome.

**Jordan:** That is the iron law. People respond to incentives. Not to speeches. Not to mission statements. To the payoff. Find the payoff, and you can predict behavior better than any personality test.

**Alex:** Here is a story that proves it. A company paid its workers by the hour, and the work kept getting slower.

**Jordan:** This was FedEx. The night crew had one job. Unload the planes, sort the packages, load the outbound planes. Simple. Except the planes kept leaving late, packages missed flights, and nobody knew why.

**Alex:** Management investigated. And what they found was perfect. The crew was paid by the hour, not by the shift. So there was no reason to hurry. The longer the work took, the more they earned.

**Jordan:** So every night, the crew stretched the work out. A job that could be done by ten took until one or two in the morning. The incentive was: take your time. The clock is paying.

**Alex:** Management changed one thing. They switched the night crew from hourly pay to per-shift pay. Finish the planes, go home early, same money. Almost overnight, the late planes stopped being late. The exact same people, the exact same planes. The only thing that changed was the payoff.

**Jordan:** Here is the thing that makes this law iron. It is invisible until you look for it, and then it is everywhere.

**Alex:** Take dating apps. They promise to find you love. But a dating app makes money when you keep swiping and keep subscribing. If everyone found lasting love in a week, it would go bankrupt. The incentive is not your relationship. It is your return visit.

**Jordan:** Or take the news in your feed. Outrage keeps you scrolling longer than understanding ever will. So the algorithm feeds you what makes you angry, not what makes you informed. Nobody decided to make you miserable. They just optimized for the payoff, which is your time.

**Alex:** Or look at subscriptions. Most software companies switched from a one-time price to a monthly fee. A one-time sale incentivizes the company to move on to the next customer. A subscription incentivizes them to keep you around. Same company, different payoff, completely different behavior.

**Jordan:** So the law has two directions. Point it outward, and you can decode other people.

**Alex:** Your boss. A rival. Even a politician on TV. Stop listening to what they say. Find the payoff, and their behavior will make sense.

**Jordan:** Point it inward, and you can decode yourself. Ask what you are actually rewarded for in your own life, not what you tell yourself you are doing.

**Alex:** Because here is the uncomfortable version. You are responding to incentives too. The reason you have not finished that project is not that you are lazy. Your days are full of small, immediate payoffs. Notifications. Easy wins. And the project pays off months from now. Your incentive structure is working perfectly. It is just working against you.

**Jordan:** Most people walk through life asking, why are people like this? Why is this company so broken? The iron law replaces all of that with one better question.

**Alex:** So tonight, try this. The next time you catch yourself reaching for your phone without thinking, just pause.

**Jordan:** And do not ask what is wrong with you. Nothing is wrong with you. Ask what the payoff is. Who gets paid for that scroll?

**Jordan:** The life you want is on the other side of payoffs that take months. The life you have is built from payoffs that take seconds. That gap is not a character flaw. It is an incentive structure. And now you can see it.
