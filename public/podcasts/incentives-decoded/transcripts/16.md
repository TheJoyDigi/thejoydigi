**Alex:** Because the tail is a signal. Only a healthy peacock can afford to grow something that useless. The waste is the proof.

**Jordan:** And humans do the exact same thing. We just call it a Porsche.

**Jordan:** And I am Jordan. This is chapter sixteen, Signaling. Last time, you learned the tragedy of the commons, why shared things get drained. Today, we look at why people spend enormous resources proving things they could just say. Costly proof. Degrees, luxury, credentials.

**Alex:** A signal is something expensive that proves something hidden. You cannot see intelligence, so you show a Harvard diploma. You cannot see wealth, so you show the car. The cost is the point.

**Jordan:** This is one of the sneakiest incentive systems running. Once you see it, every luxury brand, every credential, every humblebrag makes perfect sense.

**Alex:** The purest human example is the college degree. Employers want smart, hardworking people. But you cannot see smart and hardworking in an interview.

**Jordan:** So they use the diploma as a signal. Four years, six figures, brutal admissions. Only a certain kind of person survives that filter. The degree does not prove you learned much. It proves you could pay the cost.

**Alex:** And here is the wild part. Studies show the signaling value of a degree often exceeds the learning value. The classes matter less than the credential. The education is the tail. The diploma is the mating dance.

**Jordan:** Same with luxury. A two hundred thousand dollar car gets you to work no faster than a used Honda. But it signals I have money to waste on the drive. That signal opens doors, earns deference, attracts mates. The peacock would understand completely.

**Alex:** Here is why signals have to be expensive. If anyone could afford the signal, it would not signal anything.

**Jordan:** This is the cruel logic. The Porsche works because most people cannot buy one. The Harvard degree works because most people cannot get in. If everyone had one, the signal would be noise. Exclusivity is not a bug. It is the mechanism.

**Alex:** And signals escalate. When too many people get the bachelor's, the master's becomes the signal. When everyone has the master's, you need the PhD, or the startup, or the book deal. The arms race never ends, because the value is in the scarcity. Like the peacock's tail, it just keeps growing.

**Jordan:** The deepest cut? Much of what we call merit is just signaling. The unpaid internship signals you can afford to work for free. The prestigious fellowship signals you were already winning. The game rewards those who can afford the entry ticket, then calls it fairness.

**Alex:** None of this is conscious. Nobody buys the car thinking I am signaling fitness like a peacock. But the incentive system does not care what you think. It cares what works.

**Jordan:** So what are you signaling?

**Alex:** Your car. Your clothes. Your job title. Your neighborhood. Your vacation photos. Each one is a signal, broadcasting something to someone.

**Jordan:** Ask the honest question. Are you buying the thing, or buying the signal? The car that is fun to drive, or the car that looks expensive in the driveway? The course that teaches you, or the certificate that impresses LinkedIn?

**Alex:** And what are you signaling that you do not mean to? The busyness that signals importance. The exhaustion that signals dedication. The cynicism that signals intelligence. Some signals cost you more than money.

**Jordan:** Here is the test. If no one would ever know you had it, would you still want it? If the answer is no, you are buying a signal, not a thing. That is fine, as long as you know the price.

**Alex:** So here is the move. You cannot opt out of signaling. But you can signal smarter.

**Jordan:** One. Recognize signals for what they are. When you feel the pull of the luxury brand, the prestigious title, the exclusive club, name it. That is a signal. Now ask: is this signal worth its cost to me, or am I paying for someone else's approval?

**Alex:** Two. Find cheaper signals. The best signals are expensive to fake but cheap to produce if you actually have the trait.
