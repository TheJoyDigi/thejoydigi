**Alex:** Two people graduate with the same degree. Same grades. Same talent. Ten years later, one is thriving and one is stuck.

**Jordan:** The difference is not effort. They both worked hard. The difference is the game they chose. One picked a growing field with room at the top. The other picked a shrinking one where everyone fights over scraps.

**Alex:** Hard work in the wrong game is just efficient losing.

**Jordan:** Today we learn the most important move in the book. Choosing the game itself.

**Alex:** Welcome to Incentives, Decoded. I am Alex.

**Jordan:** And I am Jordan. This is chapter twenty-five, Choosing Your Games. Last time, in Reputation Systems, you learned how reviews and scores build trust at scale, and how to interpret them without getting fooled. Today, we go bigger. Because playing well matters less than playing the right game. Careers, markets, relationships. We will learn to choose the ones you can actually win.

**Alex:** Back in chapter two, we said the most successful people are not the best players. They are the best game choosers. This chapter is the full version of that idea.

**Jordan:** Every game has a payoff structure. Some games pay the winners enormously. Some games pay everyone a little. Some games cannot be won at all. Your job is to tell the difference before you invest years.

**Alex:** Let us start with careers, because the stakes are highest.

**Jordan:** Imagine two jobs. One is a growing industry. Demand exceeds supply. Companies compete for workers. Salaries rise every year. Mistakes are forgiven because everyone is too busy growing to blame you.

**Alex:** The other is a shrinking industry. Supply exceeds demand. Workers compete for jobs. Salaries stagnate. Every mistake is remembered because there are ten people waiting for your seat.

**Jordan:** Same worker. Same effort. Wildly different outcomes. The first person looks like a genius. The second looks unlucky. But neither is about talent. It is about the game they entered.

**Alex:** Technology sales in twenty ten versus print journalism in twenty ten. Same year. Same economy. One was a rising tide. The other was a draining pool. The people who chose the tide did not work less. They just chose water that lifts.

**Jordan:** So the question is not are you smart enough to win. It is does this game allow winning. Choose the game before you sharpen the skill.

**Alex:** Here is the deeper insight. Most people choose games by default, not by design.

**Jordan:** You fell into your career because of a major you picked at nineteen. You are in your market because a friend mentioned it. You are in your relationship patterns because they are familiar, not because they work. Default is not a strategy. It is drift.

**Alex:** And defaults are sticky. The longer you play, the harder it is to leave. Sunk cost whispers you have invested too much to quit. So people stay in losing games for decades.

**Jordan:** Choosing well means asking three questions. Can I win this? Do I want the prize? And what does losing cost? Most people only ask the first. The prize for winning the corporate ladder is more ladder. Make sure you want what winners get.

**Alex:** And the third question is the quiet killer. Some games cost everything to lose. The downside is not just losing. It is being unable to play the next game.

**Jordan:** So let us turn this around. What games are you in right now?

**Alex:** List them. Your career. Your investments. Your relationships. Each one is a game. Write them down. Most people have never done this.

**Jordan:** Now grade each one. Growing or shrinking? Winnable for you? Do you want the prize? Be honest.

**Alex:** You might find you are playing a game you cannot win, for a prize you do not want. That is not a life. That is a trap with good lighting.

**Jordan:** So here is the move. Audit, then choose.

**Alex:** One. Audit your games. Once a year, list every major game you are playing. Career, money, health, relationships, status. For each, ask. Growing or shrinking? Winnable for me? Prize worth it? Cost of losing? Be brutal. This is your life, not a performance review.

**Jordan:** Two. Pick winnable games. Favor growing fields over shrinking ones. Positive-sum over zero-sum. Games where your specific strengths matter. The best game is not the most prestigious. It is the one where you have an edge and the tide is rising. Play where you are the big fish in the growing pond.

**Alex:** Three. Quit the rest. This is the hard one. Every game you stay in costs time you could spend in a better one. The dead-end job. The unwinnable argument. The social circle that drains you. Quitting is not failing. Quitting a bad game is the first step to winning a good one.

**Jordan:** You do not need to be the best player in the world. You need to be a good player in the right game.

**Alex:** Stop asking how do I win. Start asking if it is worth winning.
