**Alex:** Welcome back to Money, Mastered. Imagine buying something that rises ten times in a year, falls by more than three quarters the next year, and then does something close to that all over again, more than once. Would you call that broken, or would you call that a pattern?

**Jordan:** That is exactly what confuses me about crypto. In chapter seventeen we learned market cycles for stocks, slow seasons that turn over years. Crypto seems to live the same seasons, only faster, louder, and with far bigger swings.

**Alex:** That is our subject today, chapter thirty, crypto market cycles. Halvings, hype, leverage, and fear have driven this young market through repeated booms and deep drawdowns. By the end you will know how those cycles tend to unfold, why people link them to the halving, and why recognizing a cycle still does not let anyone predict the future.

**Jordan:** Good, because I brought the skepticism I promised last time. Start simple. What is a crypto market cycle?

**Alex:** In plain words, it is the long swing between a period when prices rise, confidence builds, and new buyers rush in, and a period when prices fall, confidence breaks, and most people walk away. A full cycle runs from one deep low, through a long rise to a high that feels unstoppable, and back down to a low that feels hopeless.

**Jordan:** That sounds like the emotional loop from chapter seventeen, accumulation, confidence, euphoria, anxiety, denial, panic, and despair.

**Alex:** It is the same human loop, because markets are people with money attached. The difference is speed and size. A stock bear market often falls by twenty to fifty percent over a year or more. Bitcoin has several times fallen by more than seventy percent, and smaller coins have fallen by ninety percent or more, many never to recover.

**Jordan:** Why so much bigger?

**Alex:** Three reasons we have already met in earlier chapters, now combined. First, crypto trades all day and all night, every day, with no closing bell to force a pause. Second, it is a young and relatively small market, so the same amount of buying or selling moves prices further. Third, leverage is easy to reach, and leverage, as we saw in chapter twenty two, turns a normal fall into forced selling.

**Jordan:** So the cycle is the same shape, but the amplifier is turned way up.

**Alex:** Exactly. Now add the one driver stocks do not have, the halving.

**Jordan:** From chapter twenty eight. Every four years, roughly, the reward paid to miners is cut in half, so fewer new bitcoin enter the market.

**Alex:** Right. Bitcoin was designed with a fixed supply schedule. About every four years the flow of new coins halves. Past halvings happened in twenty twelve, twenty sixteen, twenty twenty, and twenty twenty four. Each one was followed, over the next year or so, by a major rise, and then by a major fall.

**Jordan:** And that is why people talk about a four year cycle, as if the halving sets a clock.

**Alex:** That is the popular story, and it contains a real mechanism. If demand stays the same while new supply falls, price has to rise to balance the two, all else equal. That is the supply side, and it is genuine.

**Jordan:** I hear a warning coming.

**Alex:** You do. All else is rarely equal. Demand is not fixed. Demand rises and falls with interest rates, liquidity, jobs, fear, and fashion, everything we studied in Part One. The halving cuts new supply, but new supply is small compared with all the coins already held and traded. The clock story also rests on only a handful of past cycles, and a handful of examples is a weak base for a law, as we learned in chapter twenty five on backtesting.

**Jordan:** So the halving may help start a rise, but it does not schedule one, and it certainly does not guarantee one.

**Alex:** That is the honest version. Treat the halving as one real tailwind, not a timetable. Now let us walk a typical cycle in four phases, so you can recognize the weather when you are standing in it.

**Jordan:** Phase one?

**Alex:** Accumulation, the quiet winter after a crash. Prices have fallen a long way and then stop falling. They move sideways for months. Headlines are bored or negative. Most people who bought at the top have sold and left. The buyers in this phase are patient, they buy slowly, and they do not talk much about it.

**Jordan:** That matches chapter seventeen. Despair seeds recovery because sellers are exhausted.

**Alex:** Phase two is the markup, the long rise. Prices climb in steps, pull back, and climb again. Good news starts to outweigh bad news. Companies build, applications grow, and each pullback finds buyers sooner than the last one. Confidence returns, but it is still cautious, and many people still expect the rise to fail.

**Jordan:** There is that saying that bull markets climb a wall of worry.

**Alex:** They do, and crypto climbs it faster. Phase three is the top, often called distribution and euphoria. Prices rise quickly, sometimes in near vertical moves. People who ignored crypto for years suddenly ask how to buy. Stories of easy riches spread. New and low quality projects raise money easily. Leverage grows, because rising prices make borrowing feel safe.

**Jordan:** And that is the most dangerous moment, even though it feels like the safest.

**Alex:** It feels safe because everyone agrees, and agreement is not evidence. In chapter twenty four we called this the point where fear of missing out replaces analysis. A useful warning sign is when the reason to buy becomes only that prices are going up and everyone else is buying.

**Jordan:** Phase four?

**Alex:** The markdown, the fall. It often starts without a single clear cause. Prices slip, bounce, and slip further. At first people call it a healthy correction. Then leverage unwinds, forced sellers appear, and the fall speeds up. Weak projects fail, some firms that borrowed too much collapse, and trust breaks. Anxiety becomes denial, denial becomes panic, and panic finally becomes the despair that starts the next winter.

**Jordan:** So the cycle is not really about the asset changing that fast. The technology does not improve ten times in a year and then get ten times worse. The price is swinging around the value because hope and fear overshoot in both directions.

**Alex:** Beautifully put, and that is the deepest lesson. In chapter sixteen we separated price from value for companies. Crypto makes that separation harder, because for many coins there are no earnings to anchor value, so sentiment carries even more of the price. Bitcoin has its scarcity and its network, Ethereum has its programmable platform from chapter twenty nine, but neither gives you a neat earnings number to lean on when the crowd panics.

**Jordan:** Let me test that with history, carefully, without turning it into a prediction. What did past cycles actually look like?

**Alex:** In broad strokes only. After the twenty sixteen halving, bitcoin rose through twenty seventeen to a then record high near the end of that year, then fell through twenty eighteen by roughly four fifths from that high. After the twenty twenty halving, it rose again to new highs in twenty twenty one, helped by very easy money and low interest rates during the pandemic years, then fell hard through twenty twenty two as rates rose quickly and several large crypto firms failed.

**Jordan:** Notice what you just did. The second rise had a huge macro tailwind, and the fall had a huge macro headwind plus real failures inside crypto itself. The halving was there both times, but so was everything else.

**Alex:** That is the right way to read history. Cycles rhyme across two layers at once, the internal crypto layer of halvings, leverage, and new products, and the external macro layer of rates, liquidity, and risk appetite from chapters three, four, and five. When both layers point the same way, moves are enormous. When they fight each other, the result is messier and the four year story breaks down.

**Jordan:** What about the smaller coins? Do they follow bitcoin?

**Alex:** Mostly, yes, and usually with even bigger swings. In rising phases money often flows first into bitcoin, then into Ethereum, then out into smaller and riskier coins, as confidence grows and people hunt for bigger gains. In falling phases the flow reverses brutally. The smallest coins fall first and hardest, and many that rose in one cycle never come back in the next, because there was never durable demand underneath them.

**Jordan:** That is survivorship in action. We remember the coins that returned and forget the long list that went to zero, so the past looks more reliable than it really was.

**Alex:** Exactly the bias we flagged in chapter twenty five. Now, a practical question listeners always ask. Can you tell where in a cycle you are, while you are in it?

**Jordan:** My guess is, only roughly, and mostly by behavior rather than by price charts alone.

**Alex:** Your guess is sound. You cannot know a top or bottom in real time, anyone who claims otherwise is selling something. But you can read conditions. Ask four questions. First, how is the crowd behaving, bored and fearful, or euphoric and certain? Second, how much leverage is in the system, are people borrowing heavily to buy? Third, what is the quality of what is being sold, are serious projects raising money, or is anything with a name finding buyers? Fourth, what is the macro weather, are rates and liquidity helping risk assets, or fighting them?

**Jordan:** Those four will not give a date or a price, but they tell you whether the water is calm or stormy, and that changes how much risk makes sense.

**Alex:** That is all cycle awareness can honestly do, and it is still valuable. It tells a long term holder why a fall of fifty percent, while painful, has happened before and is not by itself proof the idea is dead. It tells a trader, from Part Four, to size positions smaller when euphoria and leverage are high, because that is when falls are fastest. And it tells everyone to decide their plan in the calm phases, because decisions made in euphoria or panic are usually the expensive ones.

**Jordan:** So the use of a cycle is not timing the market. It is preparing your behavior before the extremes arrive.

**Alex:** Yes. Write down, in advance, what you will do if prices double and what you will do if they fall by half. If the honest answer is that a fall by half would force you to sell things you need, then the position was too big before the cycle did anything, which takes us straight back to position sizing in chapter twenty two.
