# Outline: Google Ads, The Blue Sock Way

A custom book for Long La. It teaches Google Ads from zero to deep understanding, using his own business, The Blue Sock (curated dog boarding and doggy daycare in Irvine and Orange County), as the running example in every chapter.

## House style (every writer must follow)

- Plain, friendly language for a smart beginner. Short sentences. Short paragraphs.
- Written for the ear as much as the eye: an audio version comes later. No tables anywhere. Keep lists short and introduce them in words. Round numbers are fine; explain every number in words.
- NEVER use the em dash character. Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead.
- If machine intelligence comes up, call it SI, never AI. Mostly it will not come up; Google automation can simply be called "Google's automated bidding" or "Smart Bidding."
- Second person where natural: "your sitters," "your budget," "your account."
- Every chapter grounds its lessons in The Blue Sock: real services (boarding, daycare, plus McKenna's training as a side note), real cities (Irvine first, then Costa Mesa, Santa Ana, San Juan Capistrano), real rate range ($55 to $89 a night), real roster color (Johnny's 54 five-star reviews, Olivia's solo stays, McKenna the certified trainer).
- Never invent Blue Sock services. There is NO dog walking service. Walks exist only as add-on experiences during a stay.
- The live ad account was audited read-only on November 14, 2026 (full snapshot in research/live-account-audit-2026-11-14.md). Brandon Zincone ran the account until that date; he is out, and Long now manages the account himself, working with Claude. The book may describe the live account where the audit verified the facts, and must date those claims. Where a fact is still unknown, keep using a clearly labeled example.
- Where a fact is unknown (real budget, real account settings), use a clearly labeled example ("Say your budget is $1,000 a month") instead of pretending.
- Every chapter ends with two sections: "Play It: Your Move" (a short game scenario with choices and what happens to the money, in the spirit of the Nov 6 voice game) and "The Short Version" (a five-line spoken recap).

## Chapters

### Chapter 1. The Business Behind the Ads
How The Blue Sock actually works: a curated roster of home sitters, boarding and daycare, booking requests, meet-and-greets, trust as the product. Why ads for this business are different from ads for a store: the inventory is seven sitters' calendars, capacity is limited, and every booking starts with trust. The job of an ad here is not just a click; it is the right neighbor, in the right city, with the right dog, at the right time. Sets up the whole book. Game: a click from Anaheim where you have no sitter vs a click from Irvine.

### Chapter 2. The Search Moment
What actually happens when a worried pet parent types "dog boarding irvine" at 10 pm before a trip. Intent: this person is not browsing, they are solving a problem with a deadline. How Google decides which ads to show in a split second. Clicks, impressions, and what you pay for (you pay for the click, not the view). The simple money flow: budget in, clicks through, bookings out.

### Chapter 3. The Map of the Account
The hierarchy in plain words: account, campaigns, ad groups, keywords, ads. The nesting-boxes picture from Long's Nov 6 lesson. Why structure is strategy: one campaign per goal, one ad group per tight theme. The Blue Sock map drawn in words: one account, a boarding campaign, a daycare campaign, ad groups inside each. What lives at which level (budgets and locations at campaign level; keywords and ads at ad group level).

### Chapter 4. Keywords: The Words People Type
Keywords are guesses about what people type. Building the Blue Sock keyword lists: service word plus city word, plus need words (puppy, senior, anxious, solo). Match types explained with a fishing net story: exact, phrase, and broad, and how broad behaves now with Smart Bidding. Why local, specific phrases beat broad terms for a local business (Long's own Nov 6 conclusion). "Near me" searches and what triggers them.

### Chapter 5. The Auction: How a Small Bid Beats a Big One
Every search is an auction that takes a split second. Ad Rank in plain words: your bid times your quality. Quality Score and its three parts (expected click rate, ad relevance, landing page experience), each translated into Blue Sock terms. The Nov 6 game story retold as a full chapter: your $2 bid with the perfect Irvine boarding match beating a chain's $3 generic ad, and paying less than your bid. Why relevance is the small local advertiser's only unfair advantage.

### Chapter 6. Ads People Want to Click
Responsive search ads: headlines and descriptions, how Google mixes them, pinning and when to use it. The rule from the game: the ad must echo the searcher's own words. Writing Blue Sock headlines with proof (5.0 stars, 49 reviews, in-home, one-on-one, replies in about an hour) and with the sitter names as trust signals. Matching the ad to the ad group: boarding words for boarding searches, daycare words for daycare searches. Character limits and writing many good headlines without repeating yourself.

### Chapter 7. Being There: Location, "Near Me," and Assets
Location targeting: presence versus interest, radius thinking, and the coverage rule: never pay for clicks in a city where you have no sitter. Location assets and the Google Business Profile connection. Assets (extensions) one by one: call asset, location asset, sitelinks (Boarding, Daycare, Meet Our Sitters, Reviews), callouts (In-home care, 5.0 rated), structured snippets. Mobile reality: most local searches happen on a phone, one tap to call.

### Chapter 8. Bidding and Budgets: Clicks First, Then Bookings
What a bidding strategy really is: instructions you give Google about what to hunt for. Maximize Clicks for a new campaign: buy learning and traffic. The switch: once booking requests are being tracked and arriving steadily, Maximize Conversions, and later a target cost per booking. Budgets in plain math: daily budget to monthly budget, cost per click, how many clicks a booking usually takes, and what The Blue Sock can afford to pay for a booking given a $55 to $89 night and multi-night stays. Example budgets labeled as examples.

### Chapter 9. Counting What Counts: Conversion Tracking
A conversion is the action that matters: a submitted booking request first, a phone call second. Why Smart Bidding is blind without tracking, and why the switch in Chapter 8 only works with this chapter done right. Calls from ads, call duration as a quality clue. The Blue Sock funnel taught honestly: ad click to sitter page to booking request, and the known drop-off between landing and sitter-list views as a worked example of reading a funnel. What to check when numbers look wrong.

### Chapter 10. The Blue Sock Setup, Start to Finish
The full recommended build, step by step, in words: account settings, the boarding campaign and the daycare campaign, ad groups by city and need, the starter keyword lists, the first ads written out in full, assets, location settings, starting budget and bidding, tracking checklist. Then the first 90 days: what to expect in weeks 1 to 2 (learning), weeks 3 to 6 (first patterns), days 60 to 90 (the switch to conversions and first real judgments). What success looks like at day 90, in numbers a beginner can check.

### Chapter 11. The Optimization Loop
The weekly 30-minute routine: read the search terms report, add negative keywords (free, jobs, DIY, wrong city, wrong service), pause the losers, feed the winners. The monthly routine: budget shifts between boarding and daycare, city scorecards, ad refresh. Negative keywords taught through Blue Sock searches that waste money. How to make one change at a time and write down what you did. The discipline: small moves, every week, compounding.

### Chapter 12. Growing Up: Ads at the Next Size
How the game changes as The Blue Sock grows. More sitters in a city: same campaigns, more capacity, slightly bigger budgets. New cities (north OC, Tustin, Mission Viejo): the playbook for launching a city, sitter first, ads second. A brand campaign: bidding on "The Blue Sock" itself, cheap insurance. Performance Max: what it is, when a local service should and should not touch it. Remarketing: showing a gentle reminder to people who viewed sitters but never sent a request. McKenna's training as a high-ticket campaign ($600 to $1,650 Board & Train) and why its math is different. Protecting unit economics at every stage, when to hire help (and what to hand over vs keep), and the classic failure modes of local service businesses at each size.

### Chapter 13. The Owner's Seat: Reading the Account in Fifteen Minutes
Long now runs the account himself, with Claude, so the owner's seat and the operator's seat are the same chair: he still needs the fifteen minute monthly judgment, now as a review he runs on his own account. The five numbers that matter (spend, clicks, booking requests, cost per booking request, and which cities produced them). The nine questions, asked of the account and the change log each month. Red flags in plain words: spend with no requests, clicks from cities with no sitters, one ad group eating the whole budget, tracking gone quiet. Green flags too. How this book's chapters map to the questions. Closing: the compounding idea Long already believes from Value, Created applied to ads: small relevance advantages, repeated weekly, become a moat a bigger budget cannot easily buy.
