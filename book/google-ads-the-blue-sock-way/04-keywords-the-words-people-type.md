# Chapter 4: Keywords: The Words People Type

Picture a woman in Irvine at nine o'clock at night. Her flight is booked. Her suitcase is half packed. Her dog is watching her from the doorway, and she has just realized the hard part of this trip is not the flight. It is the dog. She picks up her phone and types four words into Google.

Dog boarding in Irvine.

That one small sentence is where your advertising begins. Not with your website. Not with your logo.

Keywords are how you get into that moment. A keyword is not magic, and it is not an order you give Google. A keyword is a guess. It is your best guess about what people will type when they need what you sell. This whole chapter is about making good guesses, and then choosing how loosely or how tightly Google should treat each guess.

## A guess you can build on

Think about how people actually talk about The Blue Sock. Nobody wakes up and says, I require overnight canine accommodation services. They say, I need someone to watch my dog while I am in Hawaii. They say, my puppy cannot be left alone all day. They say, my old dog needs a quiet house, not a big loud kennel. They say, my dog gets anxious around other dogs, so she needs to be the only one there.

Your keywords should sound like those people, because those people are the ones typing.

That is why the best keyword lists do not start with clever marketing language. They start with plain words pulled from real life. Reviews are a gold mine here. So are the messages pet parents send through the site. When a customer says, I was looking for a calm home for my senior dog, she has handed you three keyword ideas in one sentence. Calm. Senior dog. Home.

A good Blue Sock keyword has a simple recipe. It has three parts, and you can use one, two, or all three of them.

The first part is the service word. Boarding or daycare. Those are the only two services your ads are built around. Boarding means overnight stays in a sitter's home. Daycare means daytime care. Keeping those two services separate matters, and you will see why all through this chapter. A person searching for boarding is planning a trip. A person searching for daycare is usually solving a weekday problem. Same dog, maybe. Different need, different timing, different words.

The second part is the city word. Irvine first, because Irvine is home and Irvine has three sitters on the roster: Johnny, Jennifer and David, and Denise, who is new and has no reviews yet. Then the other cities where the ads point: Costa Mesa, Santa Ana, and San Juan Capistrano. A seventh sitter, Trudy, hosts in Wildomar, but on November 17, 2026 Johnny decided to take Wildomar out of the ads, so it is not a keyword city today. The city word does quiet, powerful work. It tells Google the search is a match for a local business, and it tells the searcher your ad is not from some national company guessing at her neighborhood.

The third part is the need word. This is where The Blue Sock gets specific in a way big platforms cannot. Puppy. Senior. Anxious. Calm. Solo stay. One dog at a time. Each word can point at a real strength on your roster, depending on the sitter. McKenna in Costa Mesa is a certified trainer, which many puppy owners look for. Olivia in Santa Ana describes solo stays on her profile. Johnny in Irvine has fifty-plus five-star reviews, and his bio mentions a past as a vet assistant. Mariia and Nataliya in San Juan Capistrano have a bio that mentions rescue. Check each profile for what the sitter says about themselves, and let the need words follow the profiles, never the other way around.

So you build by combining the parts. Service word plus city word gives you the backbone. Dog boarding Irvine. Dog boarding Costa Mesa. Doggy daycare Irvine. Doggy daycare San Juan Capistrano. Then service word plus city word plus need word gives you the sharp tools. Senior dog boarding Irvine. Puppy daycare Costa Mesa. Anxious dog boarding San Juan Capistrano. Solo dog boarding Santa Ana.

Say those phrases out loud and you can hear the difference. Dog boarding Irvine is a person with a trip coming up. Senior dog boarding Irvine is a person with a trip coming up and an old dog she is protective about. The second searcher knows more about what she needs. She is closer to choosing. That fit between a specific phrase and a specific sitter is the most valuable thing an account can have.

## What the live account does today

Here is the real thing, dated. As of November 19, 2026, every keyword in the live account is exact match. There are 67 enabled keywords in all. Eight city ad groups hold eight keywords each, one boarding group and one daycare group for each of Irvine, Costa Mesa, Santa Ana, and San Juan Capistrano. The Irvine boarding group holds keywords like [dog boarding irvine], [overnight dog boarding irvine], and [luxury dog boarding irvine]. The daycare groups hold [dog daycare costa mesa], [doggy daycare costa mesa], and their cousins. Two general groups hold [dog boarding near me], [dog boarding orange county], and [dog daycare near me]. The square brackets are how an exact match keyword is written.

Notice what is missing. No need words yet. Senior, puppy, anxious, and solo are ideas to earn later, from the search terms report, not guesses to pile in on day one.

## Tight groups, honest lists

In the last chapter you learned that each ad group holds one tight theme. Keywords live inside those ad groups, so build your list theme by theme, not in one giant pile.

A boarding group for Irvine holds Irvine boarding words. A daycare group for Costa Mesa holds Costa Mesa daycare words. When the keywords in a group all mean nearly the same thing, the ad can speak directly to that one need, and the page the person lands on can match it too. That chain, from search to keyword to ad to page, is called relevance, and the next chapter will show you it is worth real money.

There is a temptation to build a huge list, just in case. Resist it. You are the owner, and you need to open this account and understand what each group is doing. Fifty clear keywords beat five hundred vague ones.

Also be honest about geography. Your blog can write guides for any city, because reading costs you nothing. Ads are different. Every click costs money. Tustin, Newport Beach, Huntington Beach, and Anaheim have no Blue Sock sitter hosting in them today. A keyword like dog boarding Tustin invites people you cannot serve yet. When a sitter opens in a new city, that is the day its keywords get added. Until then, the map of your keywords should match the map of your sitters. One wrinkle, which Chapter 7 covers: as of November 19, 2026, the campaign's location list also includes Tustin, Lake Forest, and Mission Viejo, added on November 11 because they border sitter cities. That is a location bet, not a keyword list. There are no keywords for those towns.

## Match types are nets

Now for the part that confuses almost every beginner, and it is not your fault. The same keyword can behave three different ways, depending on a setting called match type.

Here is a picture that helps. Imagine you are fishing off the pier, and you have three nets.

Exact match is the small net. It catches fish that are the same as the one you named. Phrase match is the medium net. It catches fish that include the one you named, possibly with other fish around it. Broad match is the big net, the one you drag across the whole bay. It catches your fish, fish like your fish, and, if you are not careful, an old boot.

That picture was always a simplification. In 2026 it is important to be honest about how these nets really work now, because Google changed them.

All three match types are about meaning now, not about exact spelling.

Start with exact match, because its name is the most misleading. Exact match does not mean the person typed your exact words, letter for letter. It means the search has the same meaning or the same intent as your keyword. If your keyword is [dog boarding irvine], exact match can be triggered by dog boarding in Irvine, or Irvine dog boarding, or a plural or a typo, because those searches all mean the same thing. Google decides how far the same meaning stretches, and it does not publish the line. That is why the live account also lists [overnight dog boarding irvine] separately. Adding a word can change the meaning. That is good news. People phrase the same need twenty different ways, and you do not want twenty keywords to catch one idea.

Phrase match goes one step wider. Your ad can show when a search includes the meaning of your keyword, with more around it. So the keyword dog boarding Irvine, set to phrase match, might match best dog boarding Irvine for a large dog, or cheap dog boarding Irvine near the airport, or dog boarding Irvine for Christmas week. The core idea is still there, in the middle of the search, but the search can be longer, more detailed, or more specific than your keyword. For a business like yours, that extra room is useful. People add dates, dog sizes, breeds, and worries to their searches all the time. Phrase match lets those longer, richer searches find you without you having to guess every single one in advance.

Broad match goes much wider. Your ad can show on searches related to your keyword, even searches that do not contain its meaning directly, using Google's signals about the person and the account. A broad keyword like dog boarding might connect you to pet hotel, or dog sitter for a weekend, or places that watch dogs. One more thing from the old days: there used to be a fourth net, modified broad. Google retired it in 2021, so you can ignore any advice that mentions it. Some of those are lovely. Some are not searches you want at all. It is the most powerful net and the most dangerous one, for the same reason. It guesses beyond your words.

## How to stay safe with a small budget

Here is the honest teaching for 2026, and it matters more for you than for a big company.

Google recommends broad match, and Google is not lying about why. Broad match works best paired with Smart Bidding, Google's automated bidding. Smart Bidding looks at each search and decides what that click is worth, based on whether searches like it turned into bookings before. With strong booking data behind it, broad match can find customers whose words you would never have guessed.

But notice the condition hidden inside that promise. Smart Bidding needs data. It needs tracked booking requests, arriving steadily, so it can learn which related searches are worth paying for and which ones are junk. Google's rule of thumb is roughly fifteen conversions in thirty days as a bare minimum. As of November 19, 2026, the live campaign's Conversions column shows zero for the last thirty days. Two Booking Requests were recorded, but they sit outside that column, in All conversions. So there is not yet a well for broad match to drink from. A small local account that is still building that data does not have a deep well for the system to learn from. And broad match without discipline has a second problem. It will happily spend your money learning. Every wrong search it tests costs you a click.

So for a small local budget, the load is carried by exact match and phrase match. That is the safe core. Exact match protects your most valuable phrases, the ones you are sure about, like dog boarding Irvine, and it is all the live account uses today. Phrase match is the natural next step. It gives those phrases room to breathe, so longer versions of the same searches can find you, once the search terms report shows you what people really add. Together they keep you close to real intent, which is exactly where a business with seven sitters' worth of capacity wants to be.

Broad match is not banned from your future. It is just not where a careful owner starts. If it gets tested later, it gets tested the grown-up way. One test, in its own place, with Smart Bidding running on real booking data, and with a tight negative keyword list already in place. Google also offers a one-click expansion setting for Search campaigns that loosens keyword matching and rewrites ad text on its own. Leave it off until booking data is real. Negative keywords are the words that block your ad, like free, jobs, or a city you do not serve, and Chapter 11 is devoted to them. Think of negative keywords as the holes you deliberately cut in the big net, so the boot and the seaweed fall through.

Two rules about those holes will save you money. First, negative keywords do not match close variants the way positive keywords do. A negative on walkers does not block walker. You must list the singular, the plural, and the common misspellings yourself. Second, a negative has its own match types. A broad negative with two words blocks only searches that contain both words. The live account has one negative written as cheap cheapest. As of November 19, 2026, that blocks only a search containing both cheap and cheapest, which almost nobody types. To block each word, enter two separate negatives. (Whether to block price shoppers at all is a separate call. Since November 9, 2026, the plan has been to let price searches through on purpose.) Broad match without that negative list is how small budgets leak. Broad match with data, automated bidding, and a strong negative list is a tool. Order matters. Protection first, power second.

You already reached the heart of this conclusion yourself in your November 6 lesson. Local, specific keywords beat broad terms for a local business. This chapter just adds the reason why. It is not that broad ideas are bad. It is that your money, your sitters, and your service area are all specific. Your keywords should be as specific as the business they are paying for.

## Why specific beats broad, in dollars

Let us use a clearly labeled example, because your real budget is yours to set. Say your budget is one thousand dollars a month. At a few dollars a click, that budget buys you a few hundred clicks a month. For scale, the live campaign spent about $419 for 99 clicks in the thirty days to November 19, 2026, which is about $4.23 a click. That is not a lot. It is a small, precious pile of chances.

Now compare two ways to spend those chances.

Spend them on a broad term like pet care, and your clicks arrive from everywhere that idea reaches. Someone wants a vet. Someone wants pet food delivered. Someone wants a job in the pet industry. A few of those people could use you. Most cannot. Your clicks get spread across a hundred intentions, and only a handful land on the one you can serve.

Spend the same money on dog boarding Irvine and senior dog boarding Irvine, and nearly every click is a person in your city, with your problem, in your service window. You have not bought more clicks. You have bought better ones.

This is the math of a capacity-limited business. A national platform can chase volume, because it has sitters everywhere. You cannot. When a sitter is booked for the holiday week, they are booked, and a click you cannot serve is worth nothing, no matter how cheap it was. A keyword's value is how many of its searches end with the right dog, in the right city, matched to an open sitter.

## The two most powerful words you do not have to buy

Sooner or later every beginner asks about two words. Near me.

Millions of searches sound like this. Dog boarding near me. Doggy daycare near me. Small dog daycare near me. These really showed up in the live account's last thirty days. It feels like you must put near me inside every keyword to catch searches like that. You do not.

Here is what is actually happening. When a person types near me, Google already knows roughly where she is, from her phone. The words are her way of saying, show me the close ones. Your job is not to repeat those words in a keyword. Your job is to have location targeting set so your ad can show to people in Irvine and your other sitter cities. Location does the near me work. Chapter 7 covers how that targeting is set.

So city keywords stay clean. Dog boarding Irvine. Doggy daycare Costa Mesa. Google matches the search to your keyword by meaning, and your location settings decide whether that person is close enough to be your customer. If you glued near me onto every phrase, you would mostly be building clutter.

The live account does carry a few broad-area keywords anyway: [dog boarding near me], [dog daycare near me], and [dog boarding orange county]. As of November 19, 2026, Google's report card gives them the lowest Quality Scores in the account, a 1, a 2, and a 3. The city keywords score better, about 7 for dog boarding Irvine and Costa Mesa. Chapter 5 explains why that gap is likely: a general keyword gets a general fit with its ad and page. Read the three subjects under each score before you decide what to do.

## Keywords are what you bid on. Search terms are what happened.

That last idea deserves its own short section, because it will save you confusion for the life of the account.

Keywords are your guesses, written in advance. Search terms are reality, reported after the fact. They are the actual searches people typed that triggered your ads. The two lists will never be identical, and they are not supposed to be. Phrase match adds words around your meaning. Exact match accepts the same meaning in different words. So one keyword, dog boarding Irvine, might be matched by a dozen real searches, some perfect, some surprising. The live account's last thirty days included dog boarding near me, dog daycare near me, day care for dogs, dog day care costa mesa, and small group dog daycare near me. They also included dog, dogs, dog hotel, dog walker, dog walker app, dog walking in irvine, dog kennels riverside, and the names of competitors.

Once a week, somebody should read that reality list. That is where you discover gold, like a need word you never thought of that starts appearing. It is also where you catch leaks. There is no dog walking service at The Blue Sock, no drop-in visits, and no house sitting. If searches for those services trigger your ads, block them, and remember the plural rule: a negative on dog walking will not catch dog walker. A click from someone who wants her dog walked at her own house is a click you paid for and cannot turn into a booking. Chapter 11 turns that weekly reading into a routine. For now, hold the picture. You write the guesses. The public writes the truth. Good owners compare the two.

That is what keywords really are. Not a one-time setup task. They are a living list of your best beliefs about how your neighbors ask for help, updated a little every week, in the words your neighbors actually use.

## Play It: Your Move

It is a Tuesday night, and you are looking at the account with a fresh cup of tea. Say, in this made-up week, the boarding campaign has spent sixty dollars. The clicks are coming in, but no booking requests yet, and you notice someone has added one broad match keyword. The word is pets. Just that. Pets.

The report shows what that one word has been up to. It brought a click from someone searching for pet food delivery. It brought a click from someone searching for jobs at a pet store. It brought a click from a family in Anaheim searching for boarding, and you have no sitter in Anaheim. Meanwhile, your exact match keyword, senior dog boarding Irvine, spent four dollars, got two clicks, and one of those clicks turned into a booking request for Jennifer and David.

You have three moves in front of you.

A. Leave everything alone. The broad keyword needs more time and more data, and maybe it will find something surprising.
B. Pause the broad keyword pets, keep the money flowing to your phrase and exact keywords, and add the junk searches, like jobs and pet food, to the negative list, singular and plural, so they cannot trigger any of your ads again.
C. Fix it with money. Double the budget so both the broad keyword and the specific keywords get plenty of clicks.

Here is what happens with each.

If you pick A, the leak keeps leaking. Broad match on a word as wide as pets keeps finding new unrelated searches, because that is what it is designed to do. By the end of the month, in this story, it has spent a little over three hundred dollars, a third of an example one thousand dollar budget, and produced no booking requests at all.

If you pick B, the bleeding stops this week. The sixty dollars already spent is gone, but the next sixty goes to searches with a service, a city, and a need in them. The negative keywords mean the jobs search and the food search never cost you again. This is the move a small local budget almost always wants. Protect the core. Make broad earn its way back later, with booking data, automated bidding, and that negative list already built.

If you pick C, you have made the leak more expensive. Doubling the budget does not fix a targeting problem. It funds it. The broad keyword buys twice as many wrong clicks, faster. Money is an amplifier. It makes a good setup better and a sloppy setup worse, at the same speed.

The lesson is the whole chapter in one sentence. Your keywords are your guesses about intent, and on a local budget, the tightest true guesses deserve the money first.

## The Short Version

Keywords are guesses about what people type, and good guesses use a service word, a city word, and a need word.
Build Blue Sock lists around boarding and daycare, in sitter cities only, with words like puppy, senior, anxious, and solo stay.
Exact match means the same meaning, phrase match includes the meaning, and broad match wanders into related ideas.
On a small local budget, exact and phrase carry the load, and broad waits for booking data, Smart Bidding, and a strong negative list.
You do not need near me in every keyword, because location targeting decides who is close, and negatives never match close variants.
