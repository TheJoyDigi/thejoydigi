# Chapter 10: The Blue Sock Setup, Start to Finish

You have spent nine chapters learning the pieces. This chapter puts them together, and it does it for a real account, not an imaginary one.

Here is where things stand. Brandon ran The Blue Sock's Google Ads until November 14, 2026. Now you run the account yourself, working with Claude. Steph advises on strategy. Johnny owns the business and the account. So this chapter is not a blueprint for some future build. It is the plan you run starting now, late November 2026, on the account that already exists. First, the account as it stands. Then each part of the build, kept or fixed. Then the first ninety days, in order, from today.

## The live account, as of November 19, 2026

Read this slowly. Every later step in the chapter points back to it.

One campaign is live: "The Blue Sock - Dog Sitting - (Search w/ Geo's)." It runs on Google search only, with Search partners and the Display Network both off. Its budget is 20 dollars a day, about 608 dollars in a full month. It bids on Maximize Clicks, with a maximum cost per click of 5 dollars, lowered from 8 dollars on November 17. Its ads show from 8 in the morning to 10 at night, every day.

It targets seven cities, using the Presence option, which means people who are in those places or regularly there. Four are cities where a sitter hosts: Irvine, Costa Mesa, Santa Ana, and San Juan Capistrano. Three are Tustin, Lake Forest, and Mission Viejo, which have no sitter but border the sitter cities. They were added back on November 11 at Steph's suggestion. Wildomar, where Trudy hosts, was removed from the ads on November 17. Trudy stays on the site. Wildomar is simply no longer an ad city.

Inside the campaign sit ten ad groups. Eight are city groups, one for boarding and one for daycare in each of the four sitter cities. Each holds eight exact match keywords, like dog boarding irvine and doggy daycare costa mesa, and each ad points at its own city landing page, built for ads and live since November 17. The boarding page for Irvine, for example, lives at thebluesock.com slash lp, slash dog boarding, slash irvine. The other two groups are general: one for overnight searches, holding dog boarding near me and dog boarding orange county, and one holding dog daycare near me. All 67 keywords are exact match.

The ads are approved, all ten of them, one in each live ad group. But all ten rate Average for ad strength.

The assets are thin. There are six sitelinks, six callouts (one listed twice), one structured snippet under the header Amenities, plus the business name, logo, and seventeen images. The callouts are all generic, phrases like Pet Care With Love and Experienced Dog Sitters, with no number or fact a searcher could check. There is no call asset, so the phone number appears on no ad. There is no location asset.

And the counting. Booking Request is the main conversion. It fires on the page a parent sees after sending a request, counts once per click, and carries a placeholder value of 1 dollar. In the last thirty days the live campaign spent 419 dollars on 99 clicks, about 4 dollars and 23 cents a click, and the Conversions column read zero. Two booking requests did show up, but only in the All conversions column, outside the number Google bids on. Enhanced conversions for leads is switched on at account level, but the site sends no customer data with the request, so it is not actually working yet.

Three old campaigns sit beside the live one. A Performance Max campaign is paused, with no spend in thirty days. A dynamic search campaign is paused, though it spent about 40 dollars in the last thirty days before it stopped. And an experiment called "Max Clicks vs Max Conv" has ended, but it still shows as enabled. It shared the live campaign's 20 dollar budget, and in the last thirty days it spent about 167 dollars of the account's roughly 626 dollars. Its old broad keywords included dog walking, which explains why dog walker searches appear in the search terms report.

That is the honest picture. The bones are good: the right network, the right location option, tight city ad groups, real landing pages. The counting, the assets, and the housekeeping are where the work is.

## Step one: the account itself

Everything lives in one Google Ads account. Keep it that way. Google's automated bidding learns from the account's own history, and a business that splits its history splits its learning.

The basics are set. Time zone is Pacific, currency is US dollars, auto-tagging is on, so every ad click carries a code that ties it back to the click. Two checks are still worth making once. Billing should have a backup payment method, so an expired card never silently stops the ads in a holiday week. And auto-applied recommendations should stay off. Google will suggest changes every week. Some are fine. None should apply themselves while nobody is watching.

## Step two: one campaign now, two later

Chapter 3 drew a teaching map with separate Boarding and Daycare campaigns. The live account keeps both services in one campaign, as city ad groups sharing one budget. Which is right for you today?

Here is the one honest reason to split them: budget control per service. A budget lives at campaign level. If boarding and daycare had their own campaigns, you could decide exactly how many dollars each one gets, and a busy daycare week could never quietly eat boarding's money.

Now do the math at 20 dollars a day. Split it, say, 13 dollars to boarding and 7 to daycare. At about 4 dollars a click, the daycare campaign buys fewer than two clicks a day. The boarding campaign buys about three. Neither gathers enough clicks, let alone booking requests, to learn anything in a month. Splitting a 20 dollar budget in two does not give you control. It starves both halves.

So stay in one campaign for now. One budget feeds one pool of learning, and while booking requests are rare, pooling them is exactly what you want, because Smart Bidding will later need about fifteen of them in thirty days, and it can count them across the whole campaign. You still see boarding and daycare separately, ad group by ad group, so you lose very little.

Split the day either of two things becomes true. One, the budget grows enough that each half can stand on its own. As a labeled example, around 50 dollars a day in total, giving daycare roughly 15. Two, one service keeps eating the budget while the other has sitters with open calendars. Until then, one campaign is not a shortcut. It is the right size.

And the brand campaign the old blueprint called for? Today your brand terms, like blue sock and thebluesock, are negative keywords in every campaign, and no brand campaign exists. Leave it that way for now. Chapter 12 explains when it is worth building one and how to check first whether anyone is bidding on your name.

## Step three: location, the setting that protects everything else

Keep Presence. It is the single most important location choice in the build, and the live account already has it right. The default option, Presence or Interest, would show your ad to someone across the country who once searched for Irvine. That person will never book a sitter in Irvine.

Now the seven cities. The four sitter cities are the core. The three border cities are a deliberate test. The idea behind it is reasonable: Tustin and Lake Forest touch Irvine, and Mission Viejo sits between Lake Forest and San Juan Capistrano, so a parent there might happily drive a few miles to a sitter. The risk is also clear: a click from a city with no sitter only pays off if a sitter next door accepts that dog. So treat it as a test with a question attached. Every month, open the geography report, the one that shows where the people who clicked actually were, and ask: did Tustin, Lake Forest, or Mission Viejo produce requests that a sitter in a neighboring city accepted? Chapter 13 makes that one of your monthly questions.

You do not need to exclude Anaheim or Garden Grove. Presence already keeps people there from seeing the ad, because you never targeted those places. What Presence cannot stop is a person in Irvine typing another city's name, like dog boarding in garden grove. That is a job for negative keywords, and Chapter 11 handles it.

## Step four: ad groups by city and service

The live structure is the one this book would build, so keep it: a boarding group and a daycare group for each sitter city. Irvine, your deepest bench, with Johnny, Jennifer and David, and Denise. Costa Mesa, with McKenna. Santa Ana, with Olivia. San Juan Capistrano, with Mariia and Nataliya. An ad group is a promise. Each one here is backed by a real sitter.

The two near me groups are the ones to watch. They catch some of the most common searches in the account, dog boarding near me and dog daycare near me. But as of November 19, 2026, their Quality Scores are poor: 1 for dog boarding near me, 2 for dog daycare near me, and 3 for dog boarding orange county, against about 7 for dog boarding irvine. A low score means you pay more per click and show less often. Do not rebuild them yet. Watch them through the end of November, and Chapter 11 tells you how to judge them.

Need based ad groups, for puppies, seniors, or anxious dogs, can wait. At this budget each would get a handful of clicks a month and teach you nothing. Instead, watch the search terms for need words. Real searches like small dog daycare near me and small group dog daycare near me have already appeared. If one keeps returning and converting, it earns a keyword, and later perhaps a group of its own. Olivia's solo stays, one family at a time according to her profile, belong in the Santa Ana ad copy for now, not in a separate group.

## Step five: the keywords

Everything is exact match, and at this budget that is right. As of November 19, 2026, your ads showed in only about 17 out of every 100 searches they were eligible for. Google also says why the other 83 were missed: about 16 because the budget ran out, and about 66 because Ad Rank was too low. In plain words, your tight keywords already find far more searches than you are winning, and mostly you are losing them on rank, the cap and the quality from Chapter 5, not on money. There is no reason to loosen the net while you are losing most of the fish already in it. Raise quality first, starting with the near me keywords.

The pattern inside each city group is the one Chapter 4 taught: a service word plus a city word, with a few flavors. For Irvine boarding that means dog boarding irvine, overnight dog boarding irvine, overnight dog sitting irvine, and premium variants like luxury and boutique dog boarding irvine. Daycare groups use dog daycare, doggy daycare, doggie daycare, and dog day care with the city name.

Broad match, and Google's expansion setting for Search campaigns, which loosens matching and rewrites ad text on its own, both stay off. They work best with Smart Bidding that has real conversion data, and today there is none to give them.

## Step six: the ads, written out in full

Chapter 6 taught the shape: up to fifteen headlines of thirty characters or fewer, and up to four descriptions of ninety characters or fewer. Google shows at most three headlines and two descriptions at once and mixes them for each search.

All ten of your live ads rate Average. Ad strength is not part of the auction, but it is a useful hint, and here the hint is fair. The city ads carry twelve to fourteen headlines, several of which say nearly the same thing. The fix is to fill all fifteen slots with headlines that each do a different job. Here is the Irvine boarding ad, complete, every headline checked against the thirty character limit.

The fifteen headlines: Dog Boarding in Irvine, pinned to position one. Overnight Stays in Irvine. Overnight Dog Sitting Irvine. Compare Irvine Dog Sitters. A Sitter's Home, Not a Kennel. Boarding From $60 a Night. 5.0 From 86 Pet Parent Reviews. Johnny: 50+ Five-Star Reviews. Meet & Greet Before Booking. Free to Request a Stay. See the Total Before You Pay. Small-Group Home Care. Optional Premium Experiences. Request Holiday Stays Early. The Blue Sock.

Listen to the jobs being done. Three headlines echo the search. Two carry proof you can check on the site: the 5.0 rating from 86 pet parent reviews shown on the city pages as of November 19, 2026, and Johnny's fifty-plus five-star reviews. Three explain the easy process: meet first, request for free, see the total before paying. One is seasonal, and comes out in January. And the price is true for this city. Boarding in the ad cities starts at 59 dollars a night, Mariia's rate in San Juan Capistrano, but the lowest Irvine rate is Johnny's 60. So the Irvine ad says 60. A county or near me ad could say 59. The old "from 55 dollars" belonged to Wildomar, which is no longer an ad city, so it is gone. Rates change when sitters change them. Check the profiles the day you publish.

The four descriptions. Overnight stays in a hand-picked sitter's Irvine home, not a kennel. Free to request. Meet your sitter before you book, and see the total before you pay. Many Blue Sock sitters care for smaller groups than a facility does. Compare reviews. Premium experiences depending on the sitter, from Sniffari walks to Pup Cup outings.

The final link goes to the Irvine boarding landing page, and the display path reads Boarding, then Irvine. Keep the city headline pinned to position one, as the live ads do. A pin starves Google's mixing a little, but the city is the whole reason this ad exists. Pin that one headline and nothing else.

Rated 5.0 by pet parents across 86 reviews.

Notice there is no price in the daycare ad. The lowest Irvine daycare rate, about 35 dollars a day, belongs to Denise, who is new to the roster, and one new sitter's rate is a fragile floor. If you want a price anyway, Irvine Daycare From About $35 fits in twenty-nine characters. Just recheck it every month.

Notice too what the ads never say. Not "our sitters are trained," not "every sitter," not "vetted." Sitters on The Blue Sock are independent, and the ads describe the platform and what a parent can compare, not a company standard.

Copy the shape to the other six city groups, swapping in each city's sitters, proof, and true price floor.

## Step seven: assets, the free furniture

Assets cost nothing to add. Here is what to change.

Callouts first. Replace the five generic ones with proof a searcher could check: 5.0 From 86 Reviews. Free to Request a Stay. Meet Before You Book. Total Shown Before Paying. In a Sitter's Home. Compare Sitters & Reviews. Each fits the twenty-five character limit, and each is true on the site today.

Sitelinks next. The six live ones point at pages like the badges collection and the story. Keep How It Works and Meet Our Sitters, and add doors that match what people searched for: Dog Boarding, Doggy Daycare, and Pet Parent Reviews, pointing at the testimonials page. For the structured snippet, the Services header will not work: Google asks for at least three values, and the platform honestly sells two. Use the Neighborhoods header instead, with the four sitter cities: Irvine, Costa Mesa, Santa Ana, San Juan Capistrano. Every value is true, and the snippet tells a parent outside those cities, before the click, that you are not in her town.

Then two decisions, and they really are decisions. The call asset would put 714-329-4534 on the ad, a tap to call on a phone. That is Johnny's line. Calls from ads already count as a main conversion, but they cannot fire without the asset, and the live campaign does not yet bid on the phone call goal. If you add the asset, add that goal to the campaign the same day, or calls will land only in All conversions. If you add it, schedule it from 8 in the morning to 9 at night, the overlap between the ad hours and the hours the site promises, so a paid call never rings after the business has closed. Decide it with Johnny, because it is his phone. The location asset would come from a linked Google Business Profile and show one address and a distance. That helps a parent in Irvine. It could mislead a parent in San Juan Capistrano, whose sitter is nowhere near that address. Think it through before you link it.

## Step eight: bidding and budget

Stay on Maximize Clicks with the 5 dollar cap. With zero recorded conversions, there is nothing for a conversion strategy to aim at, and the cap keeps Google from chasing expensive clicks in its enthusiasm.

Now the budget math, out loud. Twenty dollars a day is about 608 dollars in a full month, because Google never spends more than the daily budget times 30.4 in a calendar month. On a busy day it may spend up to twice the daily budget. That is normal. At about 4 dollars a click, 608 dollars buys roughly 145 clicks a month, a bit over 30 a week.

And here is the arithmetic that shapes the whole ninety days. To reach fifteen booking requests in thirty days on 608 dollars, each request would have to cost about 40 dollars. That means roughly one click in ten becomes a request. Today the account shows two requests in thirty days. Fifteen may simply not come at this budget, and that is fine. Staying on Maximize Clicks is not a failure. Two things can honestly speed it up. Counted phone calls, once the call asset and its goal are live, add to the same total. And better Quality Scores win more of the auctions you are losing on rank today. Raising the budget is the third lever, not the first, and it should come only after the counting is fixed, never before. Pull it when four things are true at once: tracking has matched the portal for at least four weeks, a verified request costs about 60 dollars or less, impression share lost to budget has climbed above about one in five, and the sitters have room. Then raise in steps of about 20 percent, 20 dollars to 24, then 29, a few weeks apart, watching cost per request at each step.

## Step nine: the tracking checklist

Nothing in this chapter outranks this step. Here is the checklist for this account.

One. Know why booking requests appeared in All conversions but not in Conversions. As of November 19, 2026, the answer is the campaign, not the setting: both requests were credited to the ended experiment, and the live campaign's goal list correctly bids on the lead form goal where Booking Request lives. Recheck that goal list after any change, and add the phone call goal on the day a call asset goes live. Two. Place a real test request through an ad click and watch it land in the Conversions column within a day or two. Three. Check that Google's weekly count of requests is never higher than the requests you see arriving in the portal that week. Ads are not the only source of requests, so Google's number should be equal or lower. Higher means double counting. Four. Turn enhanced conversions for leads into real data: the site must send the parent's email or phone, scrambled by a standard one way process, along with the request. That is a site change, not a setting. Five. Keep Booking Paid as a secondary action. It carries the real booking amount, which you will want later, but it stays out of the Conversions column for now. Six. Recheck all of it after any website change.

## The first ninety days, from today

Here is the plan, in order, starting November 19, 2026. Day ninety lands in mid-February 2027.

### Weeks 0 to 2: tracking first, then cleanup

Tracking leads, because nothing else can be judged without it. Around November 23 there is a scheduled check on whether booking requests from the portal now record. Do not just read the check. Place a real test request from an ad click and see it in Google Ads with your own eyes. Then put the enhanced conversions change on the site's to-do list. Decide on the call asset with Johnny.

Then clean house. Remove the ended experiment campaign. An ended experiment cannot serve again, but it still lists as enabled, it still sits beside the live campaign in every report, and it makes totals easy to misread. Removing it keeps its history in the reports. The paused dynamic search campaign can go the same way, after one look at its search terms for any phrase worth keeping as a keyword. Leave the paused Performance Max campaign paused for now; Chapter 12 explains its turn. Build the shared negative keyword lists from Chapter 11 and attach them to the live campaign. While you are in the negatives, delete cheap cheapest, which blocks nothing, and delete the phrase negative rescue dogs, which blocks dog boarding for rescue dogs, a perfect customer. Replace the generic callouts with the proof callouts above. And lift the Average ads to Good by filling every headline slot with something distinct, the way the Irvine ads show.

One routing fix belongs here too. Between November 17 and 19, 2026, the search dog day care orange county matched the boarding keyword dog boarding orange county and showed the boarding ad. Add dog daycare orange county as an exact keyword in the general daycare group, and the same search lands on the right ad.

### Weeks 2 to 6: steady hands

Stay on Maximize Clicks with the 5 dollar cap while requests are rare. Run the weekly thirty minute loop from Chapter 11. Judge the city ad groups on what they can show this early: clicks, Quality Score, and search terms. Around November 30 there is a check due on exactly that, including whether searches like dog boarding near irvine are finding the city groups. December is holiday season, so before any change that buys more clicks, ask which sitters still have room. A full calendar does not need ads.

### Days 45 to 90: the switch, when the data says so

Switch to Maximize Conversions, with no target, only after roughly fifteen booking requests, and counted calls if the call asset is live, are recorded in the Conversions column within a thirty day window. Add a target cost per request only after about thirty. After any switch, give it one to two weeks of learning before you judge it, and remember the 5 dollar cap goes away with Maximize Clicks, so watch cost per click and click count weekly for the first month.

If day sixty arrives with tracking proven but only eight to fourteen requests a month, you have a real choice, not a failure. Either stay on Maximize Clicks and push volume through better Quality Scores and a careful budget step, or switch anyway with a written exit rule, such as switching back if clicks fall by half for two weeks with no rise in requests. Do not split the campaign into a fifty fifty experiment at this size. The last experiment did exactly that, and each half was too small to tell anyone anything.

This window is also the time for one test that costs nothing but a setting. The ads stop at 10 at night, and a booking request does not need anyone to answer a phone. Once tracking is proven, try running the ads until midnight for a month, and keep any call asset on its own shorter schedule.

Be clear about one difference of opinion. Steph's plan had the switch at weeks two to four. With zero recorded conversions, a switch at week two would be blind: Google would be hunting for requests it has never once been shown. So this book puts the trigger on data, not the calendar. It is an owner's call, and you make it. Just make it by reading the Conversions column, not the date.

## What success looks like at day ninety

Here are targets you can read off the account in mid-February. They are labeled targets, not promises.

Cost per booking request: at or under about 60 dollars, with Chapter 8's band of 30 to 50 dollars as where you grow, and nowhere near the 161 dollar ceiling Chapter 8 worked out. Requests per week: about two to three from ads, recorded in the Conversions column. Geography: at least three quarters of spend from people in the four sitter cities, and a clear verdict on each border city, either it produced a request a neighboring sitter accepted, or it leaves. Tracking: no week where clicks arrived and the Conversions column read zero without an explanation, and at least one test request a month.

And one judgment test. You can say in a sentence each what a boarding request costs, what a daycare request costs, and which city you would fund next. Hit most of that, and the setup has done its job.

## Play It: Your Move

It is December 7, week three. Steph's plan says this is the week to switch to Maximize Conversions. The tracking fix landed, and the Conversions column finally shows its first real booking request, one, from Irvine. The campaign has spent about 140 dollars since.

A. Switch now. The plan said weeks two to four, and tracking works.
B. Stay on Maximize Clicks with the 5 dollar cap. Run the weekly loop, and switch when about fifteen requests show up in thirty days.
C. Switch now, and add a target of 40 dollars per request to keep costs tight.

What happens with each: With A, Google starts hunting for requests with one example to learn from. In a labeled example, clicks get pricier, volume drops in the holiday rush, and three weeks later you cannot tell whether the switch or the season moved the numbers. With C, the same blindness gets a tight leash on top. Google, unsure where requests come from, simply holds back, and the account goes quiet in the busiest booking month of the year. With B, the account keeps buying clean clicks at a known price, the count grows week by week, and when the switch comes, it lands on real history. The lesson: the calendar suggests, the data decides.
