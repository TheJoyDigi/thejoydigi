# Chapter 7: Being There: Location, Near Me, and Assets

A national chain can afford to be everywhere. You cannot.

The Blue Sock has a small roster of sitters, each caring for dogs in their own home. As of November 19, 2026, there are seven active sitters. Johnny, Jennifer and David, and Denise (new to the roster, with no reviews yet) are in Irvine. Mariia and Nataliya are in San Juan Capistrano. McKenna is in Costa Mesa. Olivia is in Santa Ana. Trudy is in Wildomar, at the edge in Riverside County.

That short list is where your map starts. If a dog owner lives near one of those sitters, an ad click can turn into a booking. If they live an hour away, with no sitter near them, the same click is mostly money spent on a nice visit to your website.

This chapter covers the one setting that matters most for a local business, how to think about distance, and the extra pieces, called assets, that make your ad easier to act on.

## Where an ad can go is not where it should go

Google Ads will show your ad almost anywhere you let it. If the settings are loose, the ads wander. If the settings are tight, the ads stay home.

Location targeting decides where your ads can show. You set it at the campaign level.

You sell a night in a real person's home. The dog has to get there, and the owner has to meet the sitter. Distance is part of the product.

## The single most important setting in this chapter

Google has an advanced location option with two choices that sound almost the same. Most beginners click past them. Do not click past this one.

The first choice is called Presence or interest. The second choice is called Presence. They are not the same, and the difference can cost you real money.

Presence or interest means your ad can show to people who are in your area, people who are regularly in your area, and people who have shown interest in your area.

Read that last part again. Shown interest. A person does not have to be in Irvine to see your Irvine ad. They might be researching a move, planning a visit months from now, or living in another state after searching for Irvine hotels last week. Under this setting, interest is enough.

Presence is stricter and simpler. Your ad shows to people who are in your area, or who are regularly in your area. Their body, and their phone, are actually there.

For The Blue Sock, Presence is the right choice. You can only board a dog for someone who can actually bring you the dog. A person in Texas who is interested in Irvine cannot book Olivia in Santa Ana for this weekend. Interest does not board dogs. Presence does.

Here is the catch. Presence or interest is the default. If nobody changes it, it stays on. Many local businesses run for months on the default and never know why some clicks look so strange. The clicks are real. Google charged correctly, by its rules. The rules were just wrong for the business.

The lesson is short enough to say out loud. Switch the location option to Presence, so you pay for people who are actually there.

As of November 19, 2026, the live campaign is already set to Presence. That is the right setting, and it is worth checking again after any account change, because the default is one click away.

## Radius thinking, or why a little bigger is a lot bigger

One common way is a radius. You pick a point and draw a circle around it. Five miles out. Ten miles out. Everyone inside the circle can see the ad. Everyone outside it cannot.

Circles grow faster than our intuition expects. Picture a circle with a radius of five miles. Now double the radius to ten miles. You might expect it to hold about twice as many people. It does not. It holds about four times as many. Doubling the radius covers four times the area.

Four times the ground means roughly four times the homes, four times the dogs, and four times the searches your ad can enter. Your budget now stretches across a much bigger world. Pull a wide circle in, even a little, and the area shrinks fast.

So treat radius changes with respect. Adding two casual miles, because it feels harmless, can add a whole ring of neighborhoods you never meant to buy.

You can also target named places instead. Cities. Zip codes. Areas that follow real life instead of geometry. Named places often fit The Blue Sock better, because your coverage follows sitters, not circles. A circle around Irvine will spill into cities where nobody hosts, and across freeways that make a short distance feel like a long drive.

In real life you will likely use both. Cities where you have sitters. Tight areas around them. Exclusions for places you do not serve, added on purpose. Every line on the map is a spending decision.

## The coverage rule

Never pay for clicks in a city where no sitter hosts.

Ads follow sitters. Sitters first, ads second.

If Jennifer and David can host in Irvine, Irvine is fair game. If McKenna can host in Costa Mesa, Costa Mesa is fair game. If Olivia hosts in Santa Ana, and she takes one calm dog at a time for solo stays, Santa Ana is fair game, with the right words so the right owners click.

If nobody hosts in a city, that city is not fair game for ads by default. When a sitter joins there, the map can change the same week. Until then, paying for clicks there risks paying to disappoint people. The next section covers the one honest exception, and how to test it.

Picture it. A dog owner in a city with no sitter clicks your ad. They like what they see. Real homes, real people, 5.0 stars. They look for a sitter near them. There is none. They book with a competitor who does have someone nearby. You paid to introduce a ready customer to a dead end.

One wasted click is cheap. The problem is that broken coverage wastes clicks every day, quietly, while the reports still show traffic coming in. Traffic is not the goal. Bookings are the goal.

## The neighbor question, and the guides that cost nothing

The rule is clean. The live account is a little messier, and that is worth looking at honestly.

As of November 19, 2026, the live campaign targets seven cities. Four have sitters: Irvine, Costa Mesa, Santa Ana, and San Juan Capistrano. Three do not: Tustin, Lake Forest, and Mission Viejo. Those three were added on November 11, 2026, at Steph's suggestion, because each one borders a city that has a sitter. Wildomar, where Trudy hosts, was taken off the ads on November 17, 2026. Trudy is still on the site. Wildomar is just no longer a paid city.

Is adding the three neighbors a mistake? Not a careless one. It is a real tradeoff, and thoughtful people can land on either side. The coverage rule is still the default. The neighbor idea is a bet you place on purpose.

Here is the honest case for it. People do not search by city line. A dog owner in Tustin who types dog boarding near me may live ten minutes from an Irvine sitter. Plenty of people will drive a dog across a city border to a trusted home, especially when the other choice is a kennel. If that is true, those clicks are not wasted. They are close customers with no sitter on their own street.

Here is the honest case against. Orange County drives can be long, and a neighboring city on the map is not always a short trip in traffic. The city ad groups cover only the four sitter cities, so as the account is built, neighbors mostly reach you through the near me and Orange County ad groups. Those had the weakest Quality Scores in the account on the same date, 1 and 2 for the near me terms. Each click costs around four dollars. A visitor who cannot picture the drive just leaves.

How do you judge it? With the geography report. In Google Ads, it shows clicks, cost, and results for each place people were in when they searched. Put the three neighbors beside the four sitter cities and ask three questions. Do the neighbors send clicks at a similar cost? Do their visitors reach the sitter list and send booking requests? And when a neighbor does book, which sitter gets the stay? Today the Conversions column reads zero, so judge on clicks and search terms first, and on booking requests the moment they arrive. The account calendar has a check on Quality Score and near-city search terms around November 30, 2026.

Say, as a labeled example, you decide the neighbors may take at most one dollar in five of the budget, and you will review them after a month. If they earn requests, keep them. If they only earn clicks, take them off the map. A neighbor city with a ceiling and a review date is a test. A neighbor city nobody is watching is a leak.

Free guides are a different bargain. The Blue Sock blog writes city guides for places beyond the roster, such as Newport Beach. Is that wrong? No. A guide costs time to write once, and then it brings readers for a long time at no cost per visit. For free traffic, a wide net is a reasonable bet.

Paid ads are different. Every visit has a price tag. So the question changes from, could this person ever matter, to, can this person book with a sitter we have, in a place we can serve, now?

Keep three lists in your head. One is where The Blue Sock can be read about. That list can be wide. The second is where it can be booked, short and tied to the roster. The third is a small, watched list of neighbors on trial. Ads belong on the second list, and on the third only with a ceiling and a review date. When a new sitter joins in a new city, move that city across. That is when ads there stop being a gamble and start being an investment.

## What near me really means

Now the two most valuable words in local search. Near me.

People type dog boarding near me and doggy daycare near me all day long, on phones, often with a trip coming up fast.

Here is the secret that saves beginners a lot of fuss. You do not need near me in your keyword for your ad to show on a near me search. Google already knows where the phone is. If your targeting is set to Presence, the person is inside your area, and your keyword matches the service, your ad can show. The location does the near me work. The keyword does the service work.

A near me search is only as good as the map behind it. With Presence on and a tight map, that search comes from a real neighbor. With the loose default on, the same words can come from someone far away who is merely interested, and you pay for the click just the same.

These searchers are also close to a decision. Nobody types dog boarding near me for entertainment. A trip is booked, or a schedule changed, or their usual sitter fell through. They want a next step, fast.

## Your Google Business Profile is part of your ads

The Blue Sock has a Google Business Profile. That is the listing that shows your business on Google Search and Google Maps, with your address, hours, phone number, photos, and reviews. The site lists your hours as 7 in the morning to 9 at night, every day.

Your ads can link to that profile, and when they do, information flows into your ads automatically. Address. Hours. Photos. The distance from the searcher.

That flow is a gift and a warning. When the profile improves, the ads improve with it. Fresh photos of happy dogs in a real backyard make both stronger. But errors flow too. A wrong phone number on the profile becomes a wrong number in the ad. Stale hours make a good business look closed.

So the profile is not a side project. For a local advertiser, it is part of the ad account, even though it lives in a different tool. Keep it accurate and keep the reviews flowing, because a 5.0 rating is proof a worried owner notices in two seconds. For scale, the site's city pages say 5.0 from 86 pet parent reviews across the sitters, as of November 19, 2026. The Google profile's own review count was not checked for this book, so look it up before you quote it anywhere.

One more date-stamped fact. As of November 19, 2026, the live campaign has no location asset, so the profile is not yet feeding address and hours into the ads.

## Assets, one by one

Assets used to be called extensions. An asset is an extra piece you attach to your ad. It makes the ad bigger, adds information, or gives the searcher another way to act.

Assets cost nothing to add. You pay when someone clicks a clickable part, under the same logic as a click on the ad itself. Google chooses which assets to show on each search, so adding one does not guarantee it appears every time. Add them anyway. When they show, they help. When they do not, they cost nothing.

Here is what the live campaign carries as of November 19, 2026. Six sitelinks, six callouts (one of them listed twice), one structured snippet, plus a business name, a logo, and seventeen images. It has no call asset and no location asset. The rest of this section walks through each one and what to change.

Assets have size limits, and Google will not accept text over them. Sitelink text gets 25 characters, and so does each callout. A sitelink can also carry two optional description lines of 35 characters each. Structured snippets work differently. The header comes from a fixed list Google provides, such as Services or Amenities, and only the values are yours, at 25 characters each. Twenty-five characters is short. Find Your Perfect Sitter fits with one to spare.

### The call asset

The call asset adds your phone number to the ad. On a phone, which is where most of these searches happen, it becomes a tap to call button. One tap. That is the whole story, and it is a big one.

A dog owner comparing sitters does not always want to read three pages and fill in a form. Sometimes they want to hear a human voice, and ask if you take anxious dogs, or senior dogs, or dogs who need medication. The call asset turns that wish into one tap, with no website visit in between.

As of November 19, 2026, the live campaign has no call asset. Whether to add one is a decision for the owner, and it has a real tradeoff.

The case for it. Calls are a strong local signal. A worried owner who picks up the phone is close to booking, and the one tap is what a phone searcher wants. Chapter 9 explains that Calls from ads is already set up as a primary conversion in the account, but it cannot fire without a call asset. There is one more catch, as of November 19, 2026. The live campaign's goal list holds only the lead form goal, where Booking Request lives. So adding the asset alone would put calls in All conversions, not in Conversions. Adding the call asset means adding the phone call goal to the campaign in the same sitting. Do both, and every real call also counts toward the booking requests Chapter 8 waits for before switching to Maximize Conversions. At this budget, that may be the fastest honest way to get there.

The case against. The number would be 714-329-4534, which is Johnny's line. Every ring has to be answered, or returned fast, during the hours you schedule. A call that rings out wastes the click that bought it and sends a worried owner to a competitor. The asset can be scheduled, so the honest version is a promise you can keep. The site lists 7 in the morning to 9 at night, and the ads currently run from 8 in the morning to 10 at night. Pick call hours Johnny can truly cover, and schedule the asset to match.

Chapter 9 gives calls a full treatment as a counted result. For now, hold the simple version. If you decide calls are welcome, the ad should make them one tap.

### The location asset

The location asset comes from your linked Google Business Profile. It shows your address area, your hours, your distance from the searcher, and a way to get directions.

It only works once a Google Business Profile is linked to the account, and as of November 19, 2026 the live campaign carries no location asset. Distance is a powerful word in local ads. There is a real difference between dog boarding somewhere in Orange County and dog boarding less than a mile away. The first is an idea. The second is a plan. This asset also helps your ad appear with map information when people search in Google Maps, a natural place for a near me search.

### Sitelinks

Sitelinks are extra links under your ad, each leading to a specific page. They let one ad offer several doors, so the searcher can pick the one that matches their need. For The Blue Sock, four doors cover most of what a searcher wants next.

Dog Boarding leads to the boarding page, where overnight stays are explained. In the cities the ads target, boarding runs from 59 to about 89 dollars a night depending on the sitter, as of November 19, 2026. Doggy Daycare leads to the daycare page, for the owner who needs daytime care while they work. Meet Our Sitters leads to the roster, Johnny with his 57 reviews, McKenna the certified trainer, Olivia with her solo stays. For a trust business, this may be the most important door of all, because many owners do not want a service first. They want a person. Reviews leads to the proof, which on the site is the testimonials page.

The live campaign has six sitelinks as of November 19, 2026: Badges Collection, Find Your Perfect Sitter, How It Works, Meet Our Sitters, Our Story, and Premium Experiences. All fit the 25 character limit. By name, none of them says Boarding or Daycare, and where each one lands was not checked for this book. A fair next step is adding the two service doors, pointed at the boarding and daycare landing pages.

Sitelinks are not four links to the homepage wearing different labels. Each link must keep its promise. Boarding searchers who click Boarding should land on boarding. That is the same relevance lesson from the auction chapter. Relevance is not one setting. It is the whole craft.

### Callouts

Callouts are short phrases that sit inside your ad. They are not clickable. They are little bursts of proof and reassurance. Keep them short, keep them true, and keep them about what the searcher is worried about.

For The Blue Sock, strong callouts sound like this. In-home care. 5.0 rated. Meet Your Sitter First. Free to Request a Stay. See the Total Before You Pay. Each one is a fact about how the platform works, and each fits in 25 characters.

Every phrase should survive one question. Can a searcher see this proven within a minute of clicking? In-home care, yes, the sitter pages show homes. 5.0 rated, yes, the reviews are right there. Meet Your Sitter First, yes, the booking flow includes a meet and greet. A claim like Replies in about an hour is only a good callout after you have measured it. It has not been verified for this book, so treat it as a labeled example, not a line to print. Four to six honest callouts beat ten vague ones.

As of November 19, 2026, the live campaign has six callouts, all generic: Comfort & Care For Dogs, Experienced Dog Sitters, Personalized Dog Care, Trusted In-Home Dog Care, and Pet Care With Love, which is attached twice. They are pleasant, but any dog sitter anywhere could say them. The ones above can only be said by you.

### Structured snippets

Structured snippets sound technical, but the idea is friendly. A snippet is a small labeled list. There is a header, a category word, and then a short list of values under it.

For you, the natural header is Services. Under it, the values are Dog Boarding and Doggy Daycare. That is the business, stated cleanly, inside the ad, before the click. One catch. Google asks for at least three values in a snippet, and the platform honestly sells two services. The live campaign, as of November 19, 2026, uses the Amenities header instead, which can hold true features of the experience. Its values were not checked for this book. A stronger fit is the Neighborhoods header, with the four sitter cities as values: Irvine, Costa Mesa, Santa Ana, San Juan Capistrano. Four true values, each easy to check, and the snippet then does coverage work, telling a parent in Anaheim before the click that you are not in her town. Whichever header you use, every value must be a fact you can prove, within 25 characters. McKenna's training lives in the negative keywords for now, so leave it out of the snippet.

Snippets also sort the audience. A person looking for a service you do not offer, and remember, there is no standalone dog walking service, no drop-in visits, and no house sitting at The Blue Sock, can see your list and move on without costing you a click. A person looking for exactly what you offer clicks with more confidence. Good assets attract the right click and repel the wrong one.

Two more assets deserve a mention. Image assets let real photos travel with the ad, a dog asleep on a sitter's couch, a backyard play session. Real photos beat any stock picture. Business name and logo assets make sure the ad looks like you, so a searcher who has seen The Blue Sock on Nextdoor or the blog recognizes the name.

## The phone in their hand

Picture the moment this chapter is built for. It is evening. A flight is booked. A neighbor in Irvine is on her phone, typing dog boarding near me with one thumb. Your ad appears, because Presence is set and Irvine is on your map.

Say the full set of assets is in place, which is the goal, not today's account. The ad is not a bare headline. It carries a call button she can tap now, if the owner chose that. It shows she is close by. It offers her four doors, Boarding, Daycare, Meet Our Sitters, Reviews. It reassures her in short phrases, in-home care, 5.0 rated. It lists the services plainly.

Every next step is one tap away. That is what being there means. Showing up complete, local, and easy to act on, in the exact place you can serve her.

## Play It: Your Move

It is a Tuesday night, and you are reviewing the map for a boarding campaign with Johnny. The campaign covers Irvine, Costa Mesa, and Santa Ana, all cities with sitters. Then you notice the location option is still on the default, and the map also includes Newport Beach, because a circle around Costa Mesa spills into it.

Johnny says the blog gets good readers from Newport Beach, and people there have money and dogs. He asks what you want to do. What is your move?

A. Leave everything as it is. The wider the reach, the more clicks, and clicks are how campaigns learn. Newport Beach readers love the blog, so Newport Beach ads should work too.

B. Switch the location option to Presence, and remove Newport Beach from the paid map until a sitter hosts there. Keep the blog guides running, because free readers cost nothing per visit.

C. Switch to Presence, but keep Newport Beach in the ads and double the radius around Costa Mesa, so you catch even more of the coast while you are fixing things.

If you pick A, the campaign keeps paying for two kinds of waste. People who are merely interested in your area can click the ad on the default setting. And Newport Beach clicks land on a roster with no Newport Beach sitter. In the story, a month of this costs you hundreds of dollars in clicks that never had a sitter to book with, and the reports look busy the whole time, which makes the leak hard to spot.

If you pick B, you fix both leaks in one sitting. Presence means your ads reach people actually in the cities you serve. Removing Newport Beach means every paid click lands in a city with a real sitter behind it. The blog guides stay up and keep earning free readers, and if a Newport Beach sitter joins next season, the city moves onto the paid map that week. If you would rather try it as a neighbor of Costa Mesa, add it on purpose, with a spending ceiling and a review date, the way the live account treats Tustin, Lake Forest, and Mission Viejo. The difference is choosing it, not letting a circle choose it for you. In the story, the same budget buys fewer clicks, and more turn into booking requests.

If you pick C, you fixed the first leak and made the second one bigger. Presence helps. But doubling the radius covers four times the area, not two, so the campaign now pays for clicks across a much wider stretch of coast and inland neighborhoods where no sitter hosts. In the story, the extra reach eats the savings from the Presence fix within weeks.

The lesson is that ads follow sitters. Paid clicks belong only where a real sitter can say yes.

## The Short Version

Set the location option to Presence, so you pay for people actually in your area, not people merely interested in it.
Doubling a radius covers four times the area, so small map changes are big spending changes.
Never pay for clicks in a city where no sitter hosts, unless you add a neighboring city on purpose, with a ceiling and a review date.
Link the Google Business Profile, because your address, hours, and photos flow into your ads from it.
Add assets you can prove and keep, and add a call asset only if the line will be answered during the hours you schedule.
