# Before You Begin

Almost everyone wants more money. Almost no one stops to ask what money is made of.

This book's answer is simple, and the rest of it is the working out: money is created value, frozen so it can be spent later. Behind every paycheck, every price and every fortune sits someone who changed something for someone else. If you understand how that change happens, you can make it happen on purpose, again and again.

That's the skill this book teaches. Not a trick, not a market tip, but the oldest engine there is: seeing what people need, making it, getting paid fairly for it, and multiplying it.

## How the book climbs

The 32 chapters are arranged in five parts, each one standing on the last.

1. **Seeing Value.** What value actually is, where it lives and why price so often lies about it.
2. **Creating Value.** How value gets made: problems solved, skill, knowledge, trust, attention and the simple act of removing friction.
3. **Capturing Value.** Why the people who make things so often go broke, and how pricing, moats, systems, brand and ownership let you keep a fair share of what you create.
4. **Exchanging and Scaling.** Trade, money, markets, leverage, distribution, networks and compounding: how value travels and multiplies.
5. **Mastery.** Giving first, reputation, choosing what to build, assembling your own value-creation system, and teaching it to others.

## How each chapter works

Every chapter opens with a story or a riddle, builds one idea step by step and ends the same way:

- **An infographic** that puts the chapter's core idea on a single page. If you're short on time, the pictures alone are a fair map of the book.
- **The takeaways**: the three things to carry out of the chapter.
- **Try this**: a small exercise to put the idea to work this week. (The last chapter skips it, because by then the exercise is the rest of your life.)

You can read straight through, or jump to the chapter that matches the problem in front of you. Either way, keep one question in your pocket as you read. It's the question the whole book is built on:

*What can I change for someone?*
