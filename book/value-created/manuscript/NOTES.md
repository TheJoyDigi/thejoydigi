# Editorial notes

What changed from the audiobook transcripts beyond the dialogue-to-prose rewrite: facts softened or corrected, and first-person anecdotes reframed (the book has no personal narrator). Not part of the EPUB.

## Chapter 1
- "posed by Adam Smith in 1776" → "Adam Smith made it famous in 1776". Versions of the paradox predate *The Wealth of Nations*.

## Chapter 3
- Bakery: "charges twelve dollars … and still earns triple your margin". With the transcript's own numbers ($3 cost), that's 4.5×, so it now says "several times your margin".
- Software: "two thousand a month … Ten times the business". $2,000 against $20 is 100×, so it now says "A hundred times the price".

## Chapter 4
- "That line belongs to Warren Buffett … in nine words". Buffett popularized the line and credited Benjamin Graham, and it's ten words. It now says Buffett "made that line famous".

## Chapter 5
- Loss aversion: "twice as hard … every time, in every culture tested" → losses "tend to feel roughly twice as heavy", and fear of loss "usually" beats hope of gain. The universal claim was dropped.

## Chapter 6
- "most startups die with great technology and no customers" → "so many startups". Loading a ship "broke everything" → "broke things".

## Chapter 7
- "The research says most people get three or four hours of truly deep effort a day" → "By most estimates".

## Chapter 8
- The Steinmetz story's year ("nineteen thirteen") was dropped; the story is framed "The story goes like this".
- "You can see it right now" (a text-to-speech slip) → "You can't see it".
- "The most valuable companies on earth do not own mines or fleets of trucks" → "Many of the most valuable". "The whole economic story of the last fifty years" → "much of".

## Chapter 9
- Cut a wrong comparison: 30,000 Segways is "fewer than a single midsize car dealership sells in a year". It now reads "almost nothing" for the most hyped invention of its decade.
- Jobs's "bigger than the personal computer" → "was reported to have said it could be as big a deal". "Over a hundred million dollars" and "about thirty thousand units" are now attributed to reports.
- The vacation-rental founders "watching conference attendees sleep on air mattresses" → hosting them on air mattresses. "Built the right thing brilliantly" → "built their machine brilliantly" (the original contradicted the chapter).

## Chapter 10
- The Nordstrom tire story is framed "As the story goes", and "has never sold tires in its entire history" → "doesn't sell tires". The usual account differs on the details; the $75 is kept. "Forty years" → "decades". "In about four seconds" → "within seconds".

## Chapter 11
- "More video is uploaded … every single minute than you could watch in a lifetime" → "in many lifetimes" (it's roughly 500 hours a minute).
- Reed Hastings "the founder" → "a co-founder" of Netflix. "The most valuable entertainment company on earth" → "One of the biggest". "We compete with sleep" is now an unquoted paraphrase.
- "An old saying in Silicon Valley" → "in the tech world" (its origin is disputed). "730 hours … a full month of waking life" → "more than a full month".

## Chapter 12
- The Tylenol story: "By the end of that week, seven people were dead" → "Within days". The lawyers' advice is framed as inference. "The most expensive act of corporate honesty in history" → "one of the most". "Invented the tamper-evident seals" → "gave rise to".
- "The two thousand dollar rule has been copied across the industry" → "held up as a model". Trust-game and trust-premium claims are hedged ("tend to").
- The chart values are illustrative.

## Chapter 13
- "In the mid two thousands" → "Back in the 2000s" (Spool's account gives no date). "The most expensive lesson ever" → "one of the most expensive".
- Greg Linden "ran an experiment" in 2006 → "described an experiment". "Amazon's 100 millisecond rule … a budget, enforced like money" is reframed as advice to treat such findings as a budget, not as a claim about Amazon.

## Chapter 14
- Korean metal type "for decades before Gutenberg was born" → "before Gutenberg was born". He took "the Asian type concept" → "the idea of movable type". "Oil-based ink already existed" → "Ink already existed". A press "in every wine cellar along the Rhine" → "in the wine cellars along the Rhine".
- Spencer Silver's glue "in the nineteen seventies" (it was 1968): the date was dropped. "The great majority of patents" → "most". "A new drug is two known molecules" → "can be". "By a factor of a thousand" → "vastly more". "Nobody said it better than Steve Jobs" → "demonstrated".

## Chapter 15
- Goodyear: 1839 now dates the stove accident, not the start of his obsession. "In debtors' prison, twice" → "more than once".
- Dropped "walked across the street, almost literally, to Microsoft" (the two companies were in different states).
- Xerox "invented the graphical interface, the mouse, and the laser printer … captured almost none of it" → "pioneered the graphical interface and the mouse-driven desktop" (the mouse came from SRI, and laser printers earned Xerox a lot).
- "The app store takes thirty percent" → "a cut, often 30 percent". Tesla's Superchargers "built those first" → "built its own". The iPod "was the first one with a store, a brand, and an ad campaign" → "what set it apart was everything around it".

## Chapter 16
- The Picasso napkin story is framed as an anecdote. The wine-shop story was replaced by what the taste studies actually show (the same wine rated better under a higher price). "Almost everyone picks the middle one" → "Most people". "This is called anchoring" → "Much of that is anchoring". The unnamed consultant's "courage tax" is now the narrator's phrase.

## Chapter 17
- "Medallion prices collapsed by eighty percent" → "by about 80 percent". The bakery customer "told a reporter" → "As one of them put it" (unverifiable).

## Chapter 19
- "In every pharmacy in America" → "almost any". "Same factory, half the time" → "Sometimes even the same factory". "Three times … most people reach for the expensive one" → "several times … plenty of people". "Economists call it brand equity" → "The dry term for it".

## Chapter 20
- The early Apple stake "worth hundreds of billions today" → "billions" (dilution). The author's "fifteen percent royalties … the publisher keeps the rest" → "often around 15 percent … the larger share". "In a garage" is kept as the standard story.

## Chapter 21
- *I, Pencil*: "saws made of steel from Pittsburgh" → "saws made of steel". The essay's opening is paraphrased rather than quoted.

## Chapter 22
- Roman soldiers paid in salt is framed as a well-known story that historians debate. *Salary* "from the Latin for salt money" → "a Latin word tied to salt". "Clipping the silver" → "putting less silver into each one". "The soldiers noticed before anyone else" was dropped.

## Chapter 23
- "Running a million auctions a minute" → "auction after auction, all day long". "Seventeen separate opinions" → "A room full of separate opinions" (17 was the winning bid, not a count of bidders).

## Chapter 24
- McLean "invented a steel box" → "turned to a simple steel box". "Moves thirty-six times more cargo" → "vastly more" (the 36× figure is about cost per ton).

## Chapter 25
- The format war: "by the time the decade ended" → "over the years that followed"; "better engineering in almost every way" → "by many accounts". "Sold hundreds of thousands of copies almost overnight" → "an almost immediate hit". "If you build it, they will come" is noted as the usual misremembering (the film says "he will come").

## Chapter 26
- Morse's "first telegraph message" → "famous". The first fax machine is framed as "Picture the very first one" (commercial fax came much later). The fax company's "nearly gave up" story is framed "as the story is usually told". "The most common reaction from investors" → "a common reaction".

## Chapter 27
- The chessboard: "the first half of the board was nothing" is reframed (the first 32 squares alone hold about 4 billion grains; it's small only next to the second half).
- Buffett's snowball: the quote is now paraphrased, and the "wettest in the earliest years" point is the narrator's, not his. "Ninety-nine percent of his fortune after fifty" → "by a widely cited estimate". "Partner for sixty years" → "longtime partner". "Doubles every seven years" → "roughly". "Scientists call it exponential growth bias" → "Researchers".
- Kept as told: the chessboard legend, the first stock at 11, and "the last three squares asked for almost everything" (they hold seven-eighths of the total).

## Chapter 28
- The giver research: the "hundreds of professionals" head count was dropped; the "bottom of every success metric … also at the very top" claim was narrowed to "the success rankings"; "one of the strongest predictors" was softened to "among the people who end up with the most, as long as they give the right way".
- The neighbor story no longer names a place or platform ("her neighborhood's online community board").

## Chapter 29
- "I know a contractor" → "Picture a contractor". "Every creator … I have ever admired has told me" → "Ask the creators … you tend to hear". "In Orange County" was dropped.

## Chapter 30
- "right when fax machines were dying" → "just as fax machines were on their way out". "Everything in this book has been a multiplier" → "Almost everything".

## Chapter 31
- "Priya, a data analyst I know" → "Priya, a data analyst". "I call it the museum" → "Call it the museum".

## Chapter 32
- "a carpenter in Kyoto I once read about" → "There's a story told about a carpenter in Kyoto". "the only asset you can give away and keep" → "the rare asset". The podcast sign-off became a book ending.

## Copy edit decisions
- Ch18: the transcript contradicts itself (Luis works "12 hours a day, six days a week", then "sixty-hour weeks"). Both now say 72 hours.
- Ch21: Leonard Read "an economist" → "a writer" (he's best known as the founder of the Foundation for Economic Education). The baker Priya was renamed Nadia, because ch31 also has a Priya.
- Ch29: "A brand is a claim you make" → "A brand starts with a claim you make", to agree with ch19's definition (a brand is what people believe about you).
- Ch16: "her income nearly doubles" is kept from the transcript. It's an illustration, and plausible if some clients take the top tier.
- The pull-quote lead-in "Here's the sentence this chapter hangs on" appeared in 13 chapters. It's kept in ch15 and varied elsewhere.
- The conclusion folds ch32's six moves (see, create, capture, multiply, give first, teach) into the five part steps.

## Conventions
- Money amounts in prose use $ and numerals ("$4 a cup"). The no-$ rule applies to imagery only.
