# Evidence register: what young people actually face

This is the evidence behind the trap list. It is not a script source: every number that reaches the screen still needs its own row in that episode's `claims.md`.

**Status labels**
- **VERIFIED**: the director read the primary source on 2026-10-01 and checked the exact figure.
- **REPORTED**: found by a research pass, source URL given, figure **not yet checked by us**. Do not put it on screen until it is verified.
- **THIN**: no good evidence found.

Most sources cut at 18 to 34 or 18 to 29. **We have almost no data on 16 and 17 year olds.** Treat the real core audience as 18 to 25 until we learn otherwise.

## What the evidence changes
1. **Trap ranking** (by how many young people it touches, verified or reported): card habits, student loans, cash-advance and payday borrowing, BNPL, sports betting, scams (many start on social media), overdraft and junk fees, subscriptions, get-rich influencers and crypto. Six episodes in the first plan ranked below these. See `01-episodes/episode-map.md`.
2. **Trust is the product.** Young people use social media for money information, but family is trusted more. Trust cues like "I show my own results" are easy to fake. So the show shows **receipts**: sources in the comments, no affiliate links, no product picks, and corrections out loud. See `content-rules.md`.
3. **Repetition and just-in-time.** One video will not change behavior for long. The Trap Files collection, the weekly cadence and a calendar tied to decision moments (back to school and student loans, Black Friday and BNPL, big games and betting, graduation and first paychecks, holidays and scams) are how the learning sticks. See `growth-plan.md`.
4. **White space.** The research pass found no character-driven personal-finance short-form show aimed at 16 to 25. This is "none found", not proof. The creators it did find are mostly talking heads.
