# Content rules: accuracy, compliance, trust

This is financial education for young people. Getting it right matters more than getting it fast. Many of these rules are enforced by `python3 02-engine/qa/qa.py <episode>`.

## Rules
1. **Education, not advice.** Say "common guidance" or "one way", never "you should" about a specific product or amount for the viewer.
2. **No promises about returns.** Any growth example is labeled **illustrative** on screen.
3. **No stock, fund, crypto or product picks.** Describe categories only.
4. **No shaming.** Mistakes are normal. Recovery is the story. People use payday apps and betting apps because money is tight or life is stressful; teach the math and the exit.
5. **Every claim has a source in a claim ledger.** One `claims.md` per episode, using the repo template `podcast/shared/CLAIMS_TEMPLATE.md`: exact URL per row, primary sources first, verdicts keep, qualify, correct or remove. **No source, no airtime.** Dated facts (rates, limits, fees, rules) are re-checked at script time. Research-pass numbers are "reported" until we read the primary source ourselves (`evidence.md`).
6. **US rules by default.** Label them. Other countries get their own cut.
7. **Plain English.** If a word needs a definition, show it in a picture.
8. **Disclaimer on the end card:** "Educational only. Not financial advice."
9. **Brand safety.** Do not name or show any real company, lender, bank, app, retailer, coin, creator or product. Fictional names and generic signs only. Government agencies and public data sources may be named as sources (for example a federal agency). In a pinned comment a company's blog may be cited as a **secondary** source when no primary source exists, and the ledger says so. Never on screen. The Joy Digi is the only brand that appears.
10. **Scams video:** teach red flags only. Never show a real phone number, link or brand.
11. **Snares stand for mechanisms, never companies.** A Snare is a trick or a design (auto-renew, minimum payments, urgency), not a lender, bank or app. Never imply all lenders or banks are bad. Borrowing has a fair price, so show the fair side too.
12. **Independence.** No sponsors, affiliate links, referral codes or paid placements from any financial product or service. No product picks. If the show ever takes money, the source is stated on screen and in the caption, and it never comes from a company whose product the show teaches about. (TikTok also limits paid financial-services promotion to 18+; `platform-guide.md`.)
13. **Receipts and corrections.** Sources go in the pinned comment of every video. If something is wrong we fix it in the video, pin a correction, and log it in that episode's `claims.md` under "Corrected" (same practice as the podcasts).
14. **Sensitive topics.** Betting and gambling: no odds tips, no hype, a help line on the end card (verify the current number and source), and check each platform's gambling-content rules first. Crypto and get-rich: no coin names, no price talk, no named people. Payday and cash advance: no shaming. Student loans: government sources only. Never use anyone's real likeness or voice. Never use the likeness of anyone under 18.
15. **No explicit engagement bait on screen.** Instagram says it will not recommend content that explicitly asks for shares, comments or tags. End with an open question, not a command.
16. **Made for kids.** Keep kid-targeting out of titles, tags and thumbnails. Set the audience to "not made for kids" on YouTube at channel and video level, and check the status after the first upload.
17. **AI use.** The cartoons and music are generated by code. **Mei's voice is synthetic (Google Gemini text-to-speech).** It is a fictional character's voice and never imitates a real person. Label it as AI-generated wherever a platform offers the toggle (`platform-guide.md`). Any other generative-AI image, voice or music needs a policy check first. Every episode keeps a silent cut.

## Review checklist (run before every post)
Automated by `qa.py`: ledger rows each have an exact source and a verdict; the end-card disclaimer and umbrella credit line are present; numbers carry "Illustrative"; no real company or product named on screen; no engagement bait on screen; the file is 1080x1920 H.264 with audio, 25 to 35 s, loudness near -14 LUFS, true peak at or below -1 dBTP.

By a human:
- [ ] The one idea is stated in one sentence
- [ ] **Every on-screen label matches the math behind it** (e.g. "a fixed $50", not "+$20", if `calc.py` computed a fixed $50)
- [ ] **The key number shows its why** in one short line, so viewers can check their own case (e.g. "24% a year is about 2% a month: $20")
- [ ] **The mechanism of the trap is named on screen**, not just its effect (e.g. "the minimum shrinks")
- [ ] **The quest is concrete**: no "a little", "more" or "try to" without saying what
- [ ] **Mute test:** with sound off, the trap, the number and the quest are clear
- [ ] **Freeze-frame test:** every paused frame has one focal point
- [ ] **Safe-box test:** `sheet.js --safe`, nothing important in the red
- [ ] **Balance test:** `sheet.js --balance`, bottom third at least 18% of visual detail
- [ ] **Device test:** a private draft viewed on a real phone on each platform, UI showing
- [ ] Growth examples carry the word "illustrative"
- [ ] Nothing implies guaranteed results
- [ ] The Snare is a mechanism, not a company
- [ ] The quest is safe, free, and under 5 minutes
- [ ] Spelling and punctuation are correct everywhere on screen
- [ ] The pinned comment holds the sources and an open question
