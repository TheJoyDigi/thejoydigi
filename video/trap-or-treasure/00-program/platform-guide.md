# Platform guide: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts

Checked 2026-10-01 by a research pass (official platform pages unless noted). **Most Google, Meta and TikTok help pages carry no date, and policies change.** Re-check the AI disclosure, finance and Made for Kids sections each quarter and before the first post. Where a figure is from advertising specs, it is marked: no platform publishes organic-post numbers.

## 1. Safe zones (1080x1920)

| | TikTok | Reels | Shorts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top | 240 px (12.5%) | 14% | 10% |
| Bottom | 660 px (34%) | 35% | 25% |
| Left | 120 px | 6% | not stated |
| Right | 120 px, and a **300 px button column** below y = 840 | 6% | 10% |

- Sources (ad specs): TikTok in-feed template, https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-auction-in-feed-ads?lang=en (we scaled the 720x1280 template by 1.5); Meta, https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels ; Google, https://business.google.com/us/ad-solutions/youtube-ads/shorts-ads/
- TikTok says the zone shrinks with longer captions.
- **The box that is clear on all three** (our derivation) is about x 120 to 780, y 270 to 1240 of 1080x1920. In our 360x640 design units that strict box is **y 90 to 416; x 40 to 320 above y 280, x 40 to 260 below it.**
- **Our working limit is y 470, not 416 (decided 2026-10-01).** The numbers above come from advertising specs (call-to-action buttons and sponsor text), which are larger than organic overlays; no platform publishes organic numbers, and Shorts' own ad spec is 480. A layout confined to y 416 leaves the screen top-heavy, so we stretch the working area to y 470 (28% from the bottom) and keep the strict 416 for the few things that must never be hidden: the hook, faces, and the quest. **This is an estimate and a risk.** The device test decides, and every scene is built so the limit can be moved.
- **Rule:** every face, number, headline, disclaimer and the brand slate lives inside the working box, spread over three bands (headline, hero, readout). Below y 470 is decoration only. `02-engine/render/safezone.json` holds the numbers and `sheet.js --safe` draws them over any frame.
- **Device test before launch:** upload a private or unlisted draft to each platform, screenshot it with the app UI showing, and compare. Our zones are ad zones and may be conservative or wrong for organic posts.

## 3. AI disclosure
- **Our cartoons are drawn by code, not generated by AI.** No platform's AI-label rule applies to them. TikTok's guidelines (effective 2026-09-24) say labels are not needed for "artistic styles, like anime": https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity . YouTube exempts non-realistic content and "fully animated" video: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491 . Meta's rule covers "photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio": https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/
- **Our music is generated by deterministic code in the render tool, not by a generative-AI model.** If we ever use a generative-AI music or image model, treat it as AI content: YouTube lists "AI generated music" under items to disclose.
- **A synthetic narrator (we use Google Gemini TTS for Mei, decided 2026-10-01):** TikTok says generic text-to-speech that is not a real person's voice needs no label. YouTube and Meta are silent. **Business risk:** YouTube's monetization policy bars monetizing "AI-generated podcast hosts offering financial guidance" (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392). Whether a cartoon host with a synthetic voice counts is unclear. **Decision: use the synthetic voice, label it where a platform offers the toggle, always keep a silent cut, and re-read this policy before turning on YouTube monetization.**
- Never use a real person's likeness or voice. Never use the likeness of anyone under 18.
- Penalties for unlabeled realistic AI content range from labeling to removal; YouTube adds possible Partner Program suspension.

## 4. Music
- TikTok **business accounts cannot use the general sound library commercially**; they must use the Commercial Music Library: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library?lang=en . Other users' original sounds are not cleared.
- Instagram's licensed library is "personal, non-commercial" for business accounts, but original audio is allowed: https://www.facebook.com/business/help/402084904469945 , https://help.instagram.com/329208821595430
- YouTube: Shorts Audio Library and YouTube Audio Library are cleared: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873
- **So:** the show owns its sound (the beat, the Snare laugh, the stamp, the coin clinks). No trending-sound strategy. That also makes the sound part of the brand.

## 5. Financial content
- TikTok bans "get-rich-quick" scams (https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/regulated-commercial-activities) and limits **paid** financial-services promotion to 18+ in the US (https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/bc-policy/en). No age gate found for organic education.
- Meta bans guaranteed or risk-free returns and get-rich-quick offers: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/prohibited-commercial-practices/
- YouTube bans "get rich quick" schemes: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973
- No platform requires a disclaimer on organic educational content. We add one anyway.
- **Not yet researched:** each platform's rules on **gambling and betting content** (needed before Ep 23) and on **crypto content** (Ep 19).

## 6. YouTube "made for kids"
YouTube treats child-directed content specially (COPPA). Listed factors include "animated characters or cartoon figures", songs and stories, and child-level language. General-audience examples include animated content that appeals to everyone and content "intended for a teen or older audience". Mature themes mean likely not made for kids. Sources: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076 , https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541 , https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654
- **Set the audience explicitly** ("No, set this channel as not made for kids") at channel level and per video.
- Our anime-style characters and "quests" carry some risk. Keep kid-targeting out of titles, tags, descriptions and thumbnails (this is our inference, not a rule). Lead with first jobs, rent, debt and scams.
- YouTube may override our setting. We can appeal. Check the status after the first upload.

## 7. Captions
No platform mandates captions on organic posts. Auto-captions exist on all three but can fail on music and silence. **We burn captions into the picture** (they are part of the design and the mute test), and also upload a caption file where the platform allows it.

## 8. Platform features to use
- **Series:** YouTube Shorts series (launched 2026-09-23), Instagram "Watch Part 2" linking (2025-08-21), TikTok Playlists. None has published an effect on growth, but they match our numbered format. Source: https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/made-on-youtube-creators-shorts-series-tv-features/
- **YouTube counts a Short view at the first frame** from 2026-08-24, and "engaged views" exclude loops, so view counts are inflated: judge by "viewed vs swiped away" and average percentage viewed.
