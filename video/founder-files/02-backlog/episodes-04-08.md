# Founder Files — Episode Backlog (drafts, headline numbers verified)
Status: candidates for Ep04–Ep08. Full scripts get written when each episode enters production.
Sourcing rule applies: every number below was verified 2026-10-09; re-verify before shipping.

## Ep04 — WhatsApp: "Fifty people built a company worth $19 billion."
- **Hook:** Fifty people built a company worth nineteen billion dollars.
- **Beat 1 (hole):** 2009. Jan Koum and Brian Acton, two ex-Yahoo engineers, build a messaging app with one rule: no ads, ever.
- **Beat 2 (move):** No marketing department. No sign on the door. About fifty employees serving 450 million users, refusing to harvest data or sell a single ad.
- **Beat 3 (payoff):** February 2014. Facebook pays $19 billion. Eight employees make over $100 million each.
- **Lesson:** Fifty people served 450 million. Headcount is not leverage. Obsession is.
- **Sources:** livemint.com (Parmy Olson), pymnts.com, bitcoinethereumnews.com.

## Ep05 — Mailchimp: "They said no to every investor. Then sold for $12 billion."
- **Hook:** They said no to every investor. Then sold for twelve billion dollars.
- **Beat 1 (hole):** 2001, Atlanta. Ben Chestnut and Dan Kurzius build Mailchimp as a side project to their web design shop.
- **Beat 2 (move):** They never take a dollar of venture capital. Twenty years bootstrapped on profits. 13 million users, $800 million in 2020 revenue.
- **Beat 3 (payoff):** 2021. Intuit buys it for $12 billion. Two founders, fifty-fifty owners, never diluted.
- **Lesson:** You do not need permission or investors. You need customers who renew.
- **Sources:** thehustle.co, davelu.com, forbestodays.pages.dev.

## Ep06 — Gymshark: "A 19-year-old pizza delivery guy sewed his way to $1.45 billion."
- **Hook:** A nineteen-year-old pizza delivery guy sewed his way to one point four five billion dollars.
- **Beat 1 (hole):** 2012. Ben Francis in his parents' garage, stitching gym clothes on a sewing machine, delivering pizzas to pay rent.
- **Beat 2 (move):** No ad budget. He sends free gear to fitness YouTubers before "influencer marketing" is even a phrase. Sales go from $300 a day to $30,000 a day.
- **Beat 3 (payoff):** 2020. General Atlantic values Gymshark at $1.45 billion.
- **Lesson:** He did not outspend Nike. He out-belonged them. Community is the cheapest distribution.
- **Sources:** oresa.co.uk, gazetteherald.co.uk, forbestodays.pages.dev.

## Ep07 — Bumble: "She got pushed out of Tinder. Then built a $13 billion rival."
- **Hook:** She got pushed out of Tinder. Then built a thirteen-billion-dollar rival.
- **Beat 1 (hole):** 2014. Whitney Wolfe Herd exits Tinder after a painful, public split.
- **Beat 2 (move):** She launches Bumble with one rule: women make the first move. It explodes: 80 million matches in the first year.
- **Beat 3 (payoff):** February 2021. Bumble IPOs at a $13 billion valuation. At 31, she is the youngest woman to take a company public in US history.
- **Lesson:** Rejection is data. She turned the worst year of her life into the industry's biggest IPO.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org (Whitney Wolfe Herd), worth.com, nextbiography.com.

## Ep08 — Patagonia: "He gave away a $3 billion company. On purpose."
- **Hook:** He gave away a three-billion-dollar company. On purpose.
- **Beat 1 (hole):** Yvon Chouinard, a climber who taught himself blacksmithing, accidentally builds a billion-dollar outdoor brand over fifty years. He never wanted to be a businessman.
- **Beat 2 (move):** 2022. Instead of selling or going public, he gives it away: 98 percent to a climate nonprofit, 2 percent to a trust. "Earth is now our only shareholder."
- **Beat 3 (payoff):** All future profits fight climate change. He kept none of it.
- **Lesson:** He spent fifty years building wealth, then aimed it at something bigger than himself. That is what the endgame can look like.
- **Sources:** npr.org (via wamc.org), nationalgeographic.com.
