01 The format

Every episode, same spine.

Four blocks, no exceptions. The hook makes a claim that sounds wrong. Three beats prove it: the hole, the move, the number. The lesson names the principle in one breath. The end card is the series mark and a soft follow prompt. No pitch, no book, no link.

    04 Visual system

    Ink on paper, drawn by hand.

    Warm paper, ink black type, and a small family of supporting inks that take turns. Heavy grotesk type at sizes that read from across a room, and a hand-drawn layer on top that annotates the story the way you would scribble in the margin of a great magazine. A brilliantly illustrated zine that moves: playful, premium, never corporate motion graphics.

    Color

    Paper#F4F1EA
    Ink#17140F
    Vermilion#E8491D

    Paper is the stage, ink is the voice, and the supporting inks take turns. Every shot sits on warm paper with ink black type. Then each moment gets exactly one supporting ink: vermilion for money and the punchline ($40, $20,000, the payoff, the lesson), indigo for the hole and the turn (2008, no users, FUNDED, the reframe), ochre as the highlighter (1,000, ALIVE, "cockroaches"). Never all three at once. The one exception is the real artifact: the two cereal boxes were blue and red, so that shot prints them in indigo and vermilion like a two-color riso pass, and its doodles stay ink black.

    Ochre never carries text on paper (2.1:1): it is a highlighter and a fill, with ink type on top. Small labels use deeper shades that pass for body text: vermilion #BF3A12 and ochre #8C5F0D on paper. Why warm and bright (show bible research, 2026-10-09): brighter images outperform dark ones on Instagram, warm tones read human and trustworthy, and neon full-bleed fatigues over 40 seconds.

    Rules

    Always

    • Numbers as giant kinetic type, counted up then slammed
    • Something moving every 2 seconds or less
    • Cuts land on the VO word, not between words
    • Key type above the bottom platform UI zone
    • One supporting ink per moment, ink black always
    • Every doodle points at something the VO just said
    • Same end card, same 3 seconds, every episode

    Never

    • Neon, lime or acid brights of any kind
    • Gold, champagne, foil, luxe gradients
    • Serif display type or script "elegance"
    • Decorative doodles that annotate nothing
    • Sepia or "vintage" filters on archival beats
    • Cheesy whooshes or stock swooshes
    • A book, chapter, cover or link anywhere in the video

    FounderFiles

    The mark is a file-folder tab in vermilion with ink type. It reads at thumbnail size and doubles as the end card. It stays crisp: the hand-drawn layer annotates around it, never on it.

    The hand-drawn layer · nine marks, each with one job

    LoopWatch this number.The $40 on the hook.
    ArrowLook here.The cereal box, the price tag, the follow button.
    Scribble strikeNot this."The cereal" in the lesson.
    StarburstThe payoff.$30,000, on paper, on the vermilion full-bleed.
    HighlighterThe word that matters.1,000, ALIVE, "cockroaches."
    UnderlineSay it louder."dying." in indigo, "the story." in vermilion.
    StampIt is official.FUNDED, worn indigo rubber stamp.
    Margin noteThe narrator, aside."40 bucks. for this?" / "same cheap cereal inside"
    SketchThe thing itself.Cereal boxes, stick-figure users, one cockroach.
    Annotate, never decorate.Every mark points at a word the narrator just said: circle the number, arrow the box, strike the cereal. If a doodle could move to another shot, cut it.
    Draw on, then breathe.Strokes draw themselves on in 0.1 to 0.4s, timed to the VO word, then boil at 8 fps (three hand-traced states) so the frame never freezes. Type stays crisp and still.
    Ink, not vectors.Wobbly paths with overshooting corners, a displaced ink edge and a soft bleed halo, multiplied into paper grain. Highlighters sit under type, like real markers do.
    05 Content first · Long's call

    Stories first. Audience first.

    Every episode that ends in a pitch turns a great story into an ad, and ads don't get shared. Founder Files is pure content: true founder stories people actually enjoy. The audience gets built first. Marketing comes later, once there is an audience to market to.

    Call 1

    No book advertising, anywhere

    The lesson states the principle and stops. No book, no chapter, no cover, no link in the video. The end card is the series mark plus a soft "Follow for the next one." That is a series prompt, not a pitch.

    Call 2

    An ongoing growth engine

    2 to 3 episodes a week, indefinitely. The series mark, a steady cadence and shareable true stories do the growing. The backlog stays at least 5 episodes ahead of production.
