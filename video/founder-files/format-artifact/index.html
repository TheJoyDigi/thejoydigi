01 The format

Every episode, same spine.

Four blocks, no exceptions. The hook makes a claim that sounds wrong. Three beats prove it: the hole, the move, the number. The lesson names the principle in one breath. The end card is the series mark and a soft follow prompt. No pitch, no book, no link.

    04 Visual system

    Built for the feed, not the shelf.

    Warm paper, ink black type, one confident accent, and the episode's own accent pair. Heavy grotesk type at sizes that read from across a room. It should feel like a well-designed modern magazine that moves: warm, human, easy to watch for the full 40 seconds, and still loud enough to stop a thumb.

    Color

    Paper#F4F1EA
    Ink#17140F
    Vermilion#E8491D
    Ep01 blue#2451C4
    Ep01 red#C21F33

    Paper is the stage, ink is the voice, vermilion is the point. Every shot sits on warm paper with ink type, so 40 seconds never tires the eye. Vermilion is rationed: the numbers ($40, $20,000, 1,000, $30,000), the one full-bleed (the payoff), the lesson's key words and the series mark. If everything were vermilion, nothing would be. Ink full-bleeds are kept for the low point (the debt) and the lesson card, so the cut has a light, dark, hot rhythm. Accent pairs belong to each episode and come from the story's real artifact: Ep01 takes the blue and red of the two cereal boxes, a cooler crimson that never reads as the series vermilion. Archival things get rebuilt flat and bold with hard ink shadows. Never sepia.

    AaInk on paper16.3:1

    Why warm and bright (show bible research, 2026-10-09): brighter images outperform dark ones on Instagram, warm tones read human and trustworthy for founder stories, and neon full-bleed fatigues over 40 seconds. Small vermilion labels on the page use a deeper shade (#BF3A12, 4.9:1 on paper) so they pass for body text.

    Rules

    Always

    • Numbers as giant kinetic type, counted up then slammed
    • Something moving every 2 seconds or less
    • Cuts land on the VO word, not between words
    • Key type above the bottom platform UI zone
    • Vermilion only on numbers and key moments
    • Same end card, same 3 seconds, every episode

    Never

    • Neon, lime or acid brights of any kind
    • Gold, champagne, foil, luxe gradients
    • Serif display type
    • Sepia or "vintage" filters on archival beats
    • Cheesy whooshes or stock swooshes
    • A book, chapter, cover or link anywhere in the video

    05 Content first · Long's call

    Stories first. Audience first.

    Every episode that ends in a pitch turns a great story into an ad, and ads don't get shared. Founder Files is pure content: true founder stories people actually enjoy. The audience gets built first. Marketing comes later, once there is an audience to market to.

    Call 1

    No book advertising, anywhere

    The lesson states the principle and stops. No book, no chapter, no cover, no link in the video. The end card is the series mark plus a soft "Follow for the next one." That is a series prompt, not a pitch.

    Call 2

    An ongoing growth engine

    2 to 3 episodes a week, indefinitely. The series mark, a steady cadence and shareable true stories do the growing. The backlog stays at least 5 episodes ahead of production.
